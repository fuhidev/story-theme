# Villain Setting Methodology

> Three types of high-IQ villain templates. Suitable for villain character design in the setting phase, to be used in conjunction with `format-specs/character-setting-style.md`.

---

## Template 1: The Chess Player - Global Layout Villain

**Core:** The villain has a global vision and multi-layered, interlocking plans. The protagonist is entirely reduced to a chess piece. Rarely engages in direct physical combat, adept at using the protagonist's personality, beliefs, and relatives/friends to set traps.

### Execution Details

- Long-term layout: buries foreshadowing at the beginning, revealing the full scheme only in the middle or late stages.
- Every action is tied to two or more layers of goals. Doesn't get enraged if plans are damaged; everything is anticipated.
- Weaves traps using the protagonist's adherence to justice, soft spots, and obsessions, creating a sense of powerlessness for the protagonist through information asymmetry.
- A comeback is only permitted relying on tiny variables the villain completely failed to predict, not by lowering the villain's intelligence.

```
❌ The villain sets a single trap, gets flustered and engages in direct combat when broken by the protagonist, full of flaws.

✅ The protagonist breaks the villain's siege plan, only for the opponent to lightly inform them that this defeat was merely a preliminary step
   to lure the protagonist into the main city's trap; he never acted personally, just tightening the encirclement 
   using the protagonist's sense of justice and nearby friends/relatives.
```

**Forbidden:** Only having a single-layer simple plan; losing emotional control after a plan collapses, showing low-level logical loopholes; forcibly lowering intelligence to give the protagonist an advantage.

---

## Template 2: The Martyr - Self-Consistent Idealist Villain

**Core:** The villain possesses a complete, self-consistent, and noble ideal that can persuade readers. Their methods are cruel, but their motives are pure. Forms a clash of values with the protagonist, making the protagonist doubt themselves.

### Execution Details

- Build a complete opposing value system, throwing out ethical dilemmas.
- Discard stereotypical weaknesses of greed. All actions serve a grand goal in their heart.
- Focus the confrontation on ideological gaming. The protagonist easily wins in combat but struggles to refute the opponent's philosophy.
- Allow the reader to develop a conflicting psychology of "His philosophy doesn't seem wrong".

```
❌ The villain simply covets power, talks empty righteousness, but privately does evil only for selfish desires.

✅ The villain firmly believes sacrificing a few innocent people can save millions, stopping at nothing to stain his hands with blood,
   not fearing death; facing the protagonist's questioning, he deduces his logic layer by layer,
   making the protagonist waver on the moral standards they've held for years.
```

**Forbidden:** Ideals are just an excuse, with the core still being greedy for power and lust; fractured logical values, with contradictory words and deeds.

---

## Template 3: The Mimic - Shadow Lurking Nearby Villain

**Core:** The villain disguises themselves as the protagonist's close friend, mentor, or confidant. A master of psychology, they precisely pinpoint the protagonist's weaknesses and moments of softness. All early goodwill and assistance are setups for an ultimate betrayal in the back.

### Execution Details

- Wears a harmless, gentle mask the entire time, actively offering companionship and help in times of crisis.
- Accurately perceives the people and things the protagonist cherishes most, using them as deadly chips.
- All early goodwill actions, in hindsight, serve the final betrayal layout.
- After the truth is revealed, layer the dual pain of the collapse of the protagonist's beliefs and interpersonal relationships.

```
❌ A supporting character abruptly turns evil midway with no foreshadowing, creating a forced twist.

✅ A junior brother who accompanies the protagonist through trials and repeatedly sacrifices himself to provide cover, considerate of the protagonist's softheartedness everywhere.
   It isn't exposed until the final battle—every time he lent a helping hand in the past was a setup to figure out the protagonist's bottom line
   and lay a trap.
```

**Forbidden:** No early foreshadowing, temporary forced betrayal; too many flaws in the disguise, easily seen through at a glance.

---

## General Core Guidelines

- Forbid deliberate lowering of intelligence or forcing low-level mistakes to hand the protagonist a victory.
- The protagonist's victory must rely on unique tiny variables and tragic sacrifices, rather than the villain actively underperforming.
- The higher the villain's comprehensive ability, vision, and IQ level, the stronger the gold content of the protagonist's final victory.
