## I. Chapter Self-Check

### Reading Fluency
- Read aloud after writing, read it out with your mouth.
- **Where you stumble = the sentence is too long or adjectives are inappropriate.** Modify until natural and fluent.

### Three-Question Test
Ask yourself three questions per chapter; if the answers aren't clear = the chapter has problems:
1. **What is the protagonist's name?**
2. **What does he want to do?**
3. **What is he doing?**

### Chapter Completeness
Looking at this chapter as independent content:
- Are there highlights/attractions?
- Is there a desire to continue reading?

## II. Character Consistency

### Core Principle
- The character's way of speaking, way of thinking, and behavior must match their persona at the current stage.
- **Prohibited:** Substituting late-stage persona into early-stage words and deeds, causing contradictions.

### Detection Method
After writing, replace the protagonist's name with an unfamiliar one and read it again—if someone else wrote it, would you still find it good?

---

## III. Transition Chapter Rules

### Core Principle
**There are no transition chapters.** A transition chapter = a buildup process to create the next sense of anticipation.

### Must Include
- Anticipation: Readers want to know the subsequent developments.
- Sense of Crisis / Information Gap: Creating tension or cognitive disparity.
- Buildup: The opponent's/supporting character's strategies and layout, and whether the protagonist is prepared.

### Prohibited
- Pure movement chapters (Protagonist goes from A to B, readers already know the destination).
- Chapters with no new information introduced.

---

## IV. Plot Transitions and Conflicts

### Stiff Transitions
**Root Cause:** Lack of "psychological transition."

**Fix Method:** Add inner monologues where you feel it's stiff, providing expected explanations for the protagonist's behavior.

### Insufficient Conflict
**Root Cause:** Neither side has a "reason they cannot back down."

**Fix Method:**
- Give both conflicting parties a strong motivation of "I must do this."
- The more concrete the cost of failure (death/losing something specific), the more readers can understand.

### Example
```
Both sides fighting for a spiritual plant:
- Protagonist: Searched for decades, only lacks this one pill ingredient to break through the bottleneck.
- Villain: Needs it to save his sister, equally willing to sacrifice everything.
→ Both sides have a strong belief of "must obtain," making the conflict sharp.
```

## V. Pacing and Buildup

### Importance of Buildup
- Buildup = Anticipation derived from early-stage suppression.
- **Insufficient buildup = Boring story.**
- You can add more supporting character side stories, but they must serve the protagonist.

### Proper Detail and Brevity
- Supporting character side stories must follow the main plot.
- Plots completely unrelated to the protagonist = invalid plots, delete them.

### Example: Romance Line Buildup
- Core climax: How they reconcile (reunite the broken mirror).
- Buildup: Root of the misunderstanding + information gap between both sides + supporting character's attractiveness/assists/sabotage.
- Everything revolves around resolving the main plot, naturally pushing toward the big climax.

---

## VI. Handling Toxic Points (Turn-offs)

### Troubleshooting Chapters with Poor Data
1. Minor toxic points → Delete directly.
2. Major toxic points → Refine controversial parts or replace the plot.

### Handling Skipped Chapters
- Skipped chapters = Poorly written transitions or supporting character plots.
- **Reduce the proportion of transitions and supporting characters;** too much will cause readers to drop the book.
- But there can't be none at all; control the ratio.

## VII. Writing Style Rules (Anti-AI Flavor)

### Prohibited
| Prohibited Item | Reason |
|-----------------|--------|
| Superfluous inner drama | Readers want the story content, not the author's conjectures |
| Direct inner thoughts | Externalize emotions using actions/dialogue/behavior |
| Endings without anticipation | Every chapter must have information intake or anticipation buildup |

### Writing Principles
- **Push the plot with actions and language,** don't pile up inner monologues.
- Readers are here for the story.
- Even transition chapters must have "content to read and information to intake."

## VIII. Text Revision Principles

### Minor Revisions (Single chapter / 2-3 chapters)
- Directly delete and modify toxic points.
- Read aloud to check fluency.

### Major Revisions (10+ chapters)
- Before revising, sort out the core direction (outline skeleton).
- Extract all important information, foreshadowing, and settings.
- **When rewriting, you must put the information back,** otherwise the logic is disjointed.
- The persona must match the current stage, not the late stage.

### Core
If you can't handle the subsequent plot connections, **do not revise;** just finish writing the rest well.

## IX. Pacing Judgment

### Pacing is Too Fast
**Judgment:** If readers need dozens of sentences to summarize after reading, or can't digest it at all → pacing is too fast.

**Symptoms:**
- No breathing room to digest information.
- Emotional buildup is inadequate.
- Readers skip over foreshadowing content without seeing it.

### Pacing is Too Slow
**Judgment:** If a reader can summarize the entire chapter in one sentence → pacing is too slow.

**Symptoms:**
- "Protagonist postures in front of XX family, various environmental changes, bystanders shocked, psychological descriptions."
- No substantial story plot progression.
- Readers summarize the core of the whole chapter in one sentence.

### Quantitative Method
Look at horizontal comparisons with popular books of the same type:
- 1 chapter of a popular book = how many plot points?
- Writing a non-protagonist plot unrelated to the main line for 10+ chapters = bloated and cumbersome.

---

## X. Pacing Adjustment

### Pacing Too Fast → Add Emotion
- Fast pacing accompanies thrilling plots (fights/crises/rescues).
- Find ways to mobilize the reader's emotions.
- **Emotional buildup + explosion = slowing down the story.**

### Pacing Too Slow → Add Hooks
- If the ending hook is placed beautifully, readers won't feel it's filler.
- They will think it's building up for the upcoming plot.

### Pacing Too Slow → Cut Plots
Cut three types:
1. Plots unrelated to the male and female leads' development.
2. Plots too far from the main line.
3. Plots that don't drive the story forward.

**Proper Detail and Brevity:** Sacrificing some plot to achieve a goal can linearly improve the beauty of the story afterward.

## XI. Chapter Revision Judgment

### Revise Only the Chapter
- Pacing issues (too fast/too slow)
- Stiff dialogue
- Too much or too little description
- Inadequate emotion

### Needs Overall Structural Changes
- Main line logic breaks
- Character settings are inconsistent
- Major plot direction changes

### Judgment Method
1. Look at data: The point where the chapter completion rate drops → start revising from the previous chapter.
2. Self-analysis: Did the persona collapse? Or is the plot itself unattractive?

### Revision Principles
- Keep the core content information of the original text unchanged.
- Only solve the specific content causing reader loss.
- **Do not over-pursue perfection;** solving self-check problems + smooth text is enough.

## XII. Plot Enrichment

### Thin vs. Rich
| Type | Characteristics |
|------|-----------------|
| Thin | Plot jumps (A→B, conflict→resolution), lacking transition |
| Rich | Continuous, every core plot has buildup/process/aftermath |

### Buildup
- Use detail hints, character foreshadowing, environmental contrast.
- Make readers "suddenly realize."
- Avoid useless details to prevent information clutter.

### Process
- Don't directly describe the matter as completed.
- Detailed steps: attempt → failure → struggle → growth.
- Layer by layer pushing to the climax.
- Too many details will drag.

### Aftermath
Every event must have subsequent impacts:
- Character emotional changes
- Relationship changes
- Subsequent hidden dangers
- New conflicts

## XIII. Atmosphere Enrichment

### Function of Environment Description
- Highlight character emotions
- Render atmosphere
- Give the scene a visual sense
- Enrich story texture

### Genre Focus
| Genre | Environment Description Focus |
|-------|-----------------------------|
| Fantasy | Heaven and earth phenomena, sect scenery |
| Suspense | Dark, oppressive |
| Romance | Delicate, atmospheric |
| Realistic | Close to life, slice-of-life (fireworks feel) |

### Pitfalls
- Environment descriptions over 100 words must be checked for filler.
- Environment descriptions must fit character emotions and plot.

## XIV. Pacing Enrichment

### Core: Tension and Relaxation
- Always fast → readers get tired
- Always slow → readers get bored
- Have tension (conflicts/climaxes) and relaxation (daily life/memories/solitude)

### Five Techniques
1. **Detail climaxes:** Detailed scenes, highlight key points.
2. **Intersperse daily life:** Lightweight and warm, alternating loose and tight (but can't be a laundry list).
3. **Moderate reversals:** Add layers, but shouldn't be frequent.
4. **Appropriate extension:** Don't repeatedly write around just a few points.
5. **Dig deep into foreshadowing:** Personas/backgrounds can have multiple facets.

### Warm Daily Life Creation
- **Dialogue fits persona:** Lively → straightforward and bouncy, Calm → gentle and restrained.
- **Combined with scenes:** Eating/cooking/getting off work/shopping, happening naturally.
- **Small interactions:** Complaining/teasing/little tsundere moments/small actions.
- **Hide warmth:** Revealed through details, no need to say it explicitly.

### Pitfalls
- Daily dialogues should be short and snappy; overly long ones easily become filler.
- Do not force daily life into the middle of tense conflicts.

## XV. Character Shaping

### Core Principles
- Characters are not "props" serving the plot.
- Avoid stereotyping and simplification.
- Add a complex, multi-faceted "living person feel."

### Fill in the Past
Use flashbacks/foreshadowing to explain character motives, interspersed multiple times in the plot, not entirely revealed all at once:

| ❌ Thin | ✅ Rich |
|---------|---------|
| Female lead rejects milk | Female lead sees milk → recalls stepmother forcing sour milk → fingers tremble → rejects |

### Add Details
Use actions/expressions/psychology to portray emotions, do not state emotions directly:

| ❌ Direct Statement | ✅ Visual Scene |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| He asked: "You won't lie to me, right?" | Seeing her tightly clenched fingers in her cuff → the temperature in his eyes dropped a few degrees → tone remained normal |

### Add Growth
Growth logic: Dilemma → struggle/reflection → change/breakthrough → settling/transformation

Three types of growth:
- **Protagonist:** Core growth line (cowardly → brave, selfish → responsible)
- **Supporting:** Auxiliary growth line (foil to the protagonist, drives the main line)
- **Villain:** Reverse growth line (not whitewashing, but making actions more logical)

### Endow with Soul (Supporting Characters)
1. **Establish Persona:** Core tag + 1-2 auxiliary details, making them recognizable.
2. **Endow Desire:** Has goals independent of the protagonist.
3. **Link to Main Line:** Drives/supplements/foils the main line.
4. **Leave Blanks:** Don't write completely full; leave room for readers to savor.

### Pitfalls
- Growth cannot be "disconnected": A cowardly person won't suddenly fear nothing.
- Supporting characters shouldn't steal the protagonist's spotlight.
- Do not forcibly whitewash/blacken; changes must be logical.

## XVI. Opening Rules

### The First Page is More Important Than the First Three Chapters
If readers don't see exciting content on the first page, they will swipe away directly, and the completion rate for the rest will be poor.

### Cut to the Chase on the First Page
| ❌ Background Intro Opening | ✅ Conflict/Pain Point Opening |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| "I am from XX, obtained a system..." | Directly throw out conflict/suspense/pain point |
| Readers don't know what to expect | Readers immediately have anticipation |

### Opening Techniques
1. **Contrast/Drama:** Hook them with the very first sentence.
2. **Pain point/Resonance:** Give readers a sense of immersion.
3. **Anticipation:** The end of the first page must make the reader want to turn to the next page.

### End-of-Chapter Hooks
- Leave room for imagination at the end of every chapter.
- Don't just wait for readers to turn the page, make them **want to turn the page**.
- Hook examples: Use an interesting conversation/conflict as an ending to trigger anticipation for the next chapter.

## XVII. Golden Finger (Cheat) Design

### Innovativeness
| ❌ Traditional Golden Finger | ✅ Innovative Golden Finger |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| Ring grandpa/mysterious space/direct panel binding | Something providing help invisibly |
| Readers find it dull | Drives the plot but doesn't pad word count |

### Features of a Good Golden Finger
1. Doesn't have to be a system; can be any form.
2. **Gradually drives** the protagonist's strength up, not all in one step.
3. Every upgrade creates anticipation.
4. Participates in plot progression, doesn't just appear as a tool.

### Pitfalls
- System appearing frequently = padding word count = reader loss.
- Golden finger not participating in the plot = pale and weak.

## XVIII. Mid-Term Plot Breakthrough

### Burying Foreshadowing
- Bury foreshadowing within the first tens of thousands of words.
- Reveal it hundreds of thousands of words later for a minor climax.
- Foreshadowing can be: narration/background intro/a character's sentence.

### Types of Foreshadowing
1. **Linguistic Foreshadowing:** A character's line (understood later).
2. **Environmental Foreshadowing:** Detail descriptions (used for deduction later).
3. Bury one every tens of thousands to a hundred thousand words, loop after revealing.

### Rational Use of Supporting Characters
- Supporting character dies = generates new conflict = new plot.
- Only when people appear/disappear do plots emerge.
- Reluctant to let characters die = readers forget supporting characters + logic loopholes.

### Pitfalls
- Cannot change maps (Urban genres cannot change countries).
- Forcibly changing maps = sense of disconnect = reader loss.

## XIX. Genre Selection

### Veteran Authors: Cultivate Strong Genres
- Micro-innovate settings within familiar categories.
- Reader demographics are different, worldviews are different.
- Ex: Era novel → Era substitute bride / Era sisters marrying together / Era rebirth revenge.

### New Authors: Start from Hobbies
- Choose the type you read the most.
- Understand the writing style/personas/plots of this type.
- **Readers turning into authors are the easiest to succeed.**

### Pitfalls
- Changing categories = changing reader demographic = lower success rate.
- Unfamiliar types easily lead to writer's block.

## XX. The Second Book is Worse Than the First

### Core Misconception
**Over-pursuing a "premium feel," ignoring the foreground story.**

### Foreground Story vs. Background Story
| Type | Content |
|------|---------|
| Foreground Story | Points that attract readers at the start (conflicts/confrontations/conquest targets) |
| Background Story | Massive worldview, deep persona excavation |

### Key
- Write a good-looking foreground story first.
- A lofty background story is useless if readers can't get into it.
- **Refine based on the popular text, don't abandon successful experience.**

## XXI. Innovation Ratio

### Market Reality
- New readers constantly influx; "old tropes" are still new settings to them.
- No one is eighteen forever, but there are always eighteen-year-olds.
- Pure trope competition is fierce; hard to stand out without strong writing skills.

### Safe Innovation
**Old wine in a new bottle:** Micro-innovation based on tropes.

### Innovation Ratio Suggestions
| Stage | Innovation Ratio |
|-------|------------------|
| Novice | 10% innovation + 90% tropes |
| Expert | 20% innovation + 80% tropes |
| First write a hit book | Then gradually increase the innovation ratio |

### Pitfalls
- Innovating too drastically = poor results = affects writing morale.
- Don't take steps too big; proceed steadily.

---

## XXII. Learning from Popular Texts

### Avoid Imitation
- Don't just read one book.
- Find 2-3 books of the same type, compare commonalities.
- Commonalities = tropes, you can write them.
- The entire storyline being similar = continuous trope collision = plagiarism.

### Essence of Learning
The **most essential characteristics** to learn from popular texts:
- Why the persona is likable.
- Whether material selection is drawing aggro or warm leveling up.
- How to create anticipation.

**You are not learning the plot, you are learning writing methods.**

### Complete Reading
- The essence is not only in the opening.
- Reading a complete million-word long novel is more useful than reading ten openings.
- Complete reading = enhanced long-form control ability = reduced rate of story collapse.

## XXIII. Preventing Story Collapse

### Common Causes of Story Collapse
1. **After a suspense is revealed, no new suspense follows up.**
2. **Map changing is not smooth enough, severe sense of disconnect.**

### Prevention Methods
**Alternate suspense buildup in advance:**
- Before the 1st suspense is revealed, you must build up the 2nd suspense.
- It's not "build up right before ending," but "intersperse the 2nd while writing the 1st."
- Keep an average of over 1.75 hooks per chapter.

**Map changing should focus on the story:**
- Write "protagonist gets the item," don't write "psychological journey and environment descriptions on the road."
- Downplay the map change, let readers unknowingly complete the event with the protagonist.

**Newcomers shouldn't change maps temporarily:**
- School novels keep the whole book in school.
- Inner court struggle novels keep the whole book in the mansion.
- If you must change, drag it out as late as possible.

## XXIV. Handling Negative Reviews

### Core Mindset
- Criticism is a way to grow.
- But reader demands ≠ changes you need to make.

### Judgment Standards
| Can Reference | Do Not Reference |
|---------------|------------------|
| Common sense errors | Interfering with main plot logic |
| Advice during data drops | Emotional negative reviews |

### Pitfalls
- Don't be fooled by readers' "sweet talk."
- They run away after getting their thrill; only the author cries.
- Cramming 300k words of plot into 100k words = completely messed up pacing = story collapse.

## XXV. Perspective Rules

### God's-Eye View (Omniscient) Problems
- Poor reader immersion.
- Perspective jumping between supporting characters = sense of disconnect.
- Lowers the immersion of the novel.

### Perspective Focus
**Early stage focus on protagonist's perspective:**
- The first tens of thousands of words revolve around the protagonist alone.
- Things happening to others are briefly summarized or heard from others.
- The protagonist must be present.

**Late stage can add a small amount:**
- After reader immersion is established.
- Keep it within a few hundred words.
- Inappropriate to spend large chunks talking about supporting characters.

### Pitfalls
- When supporting character settings aren't as brilliant as the protagonist's, talking about them = risk of dropping the book.
- Omniscient perspective makes deep immersion difficult.

## XXVI. Timeline Settings

### Five Timeline Types
| Type | Characteristics | Applicable Genres |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Chronological | Arrow-style, clear causality | Most genres |
| Circular | Cut in from the middle, large blank space earlier | Instances/Suspense/Ancient Romance |
| Save/Load | Node choice → branching endings | Apocalyptic/Game/Romance Salvation |
| Pure Loop | Overall loop, no exit | Suspense/Sci-Fi |
| Multi-thread | Combination of the above | Massive worldview |

### Chronological Timeline
- Progresses by cause → process → result.
- Clear organization, easy to pick up.
- First choice for newcomers.

### Circular Timeline
**Cut in from the middle:** There have been countless loops before, left unmentioned.

**Advantages:**
- Large blank space, can bury countless foreshadowings.
- Hidden clues scattered everywhere.
- Three timelines: Past (foreshadowing) + Present (progression) + Future (suspense).

**Applicable:** Deduction suspense, Cyber, Sci-Fi, Ancient Romance.

**Ancient Romance Application Example:**
- Opens with a loving couple getting a divorce.
- The preceding story (hidden line) + current state (open line) + subsequent separation/reunion (suspense).

### Save/Load Timeline
**Node choice → different endings:** Wrong choice → rollback (load save) → re-choose.

**Advantages:**
- Failure is no longer a toxic point.
- Rollback = precognition golden finger.
- Ten plot nodes can extend many stories.

**Apocalyptic Match:**
```
Plot a success → Plot b killed → Rollback → Change route → Plot e
```

**Instance Match:** A single instance repeatedly looping until escaping the instance.

## XXVII. Traditional Xuanhuan Breakthrough

### Core Problem
Too much existing stock, severe homogenization, reader aesthetic fatigue.

### Breakthrough Method: Old Wine in New Bottles
**Micro-innovation** in three directions:
1. **Persona Innovation:** Design plot around core persona.
   - Ex: Trash persona → Must be "more trash" to join the team.
   - Ex: Trash → Is instead the sole candidate for the ancient cave inheritance.
2. **Golden Finger Innovation:** Give readers invincibility + restrictions.
   - Ex: Pagoda type (treasures hidden on each floor, anticipation layer by layer).
   - Avoid: Too invincible → plot collapse.
3. **Worldview Innovation:** Cultivation system/world rules.
   - Ex: Only strong bloodlines can cultivate, ordinary bloodlines are disqualified.

### Pitfalls
- Newcomers shouldn't innovate on the cultivation system; keep it easy for readers to immerse.
- World operation rules must be able to cause emotional resonance.

## XXXII. Emotional Progression

### Core Principle
**Don't write the event; write the emotion expressed by the person.**

| ❌ Write Event | ✅ Write Emotion |
|----------------|------------------|
| Protagonist's family is slaughtered, very despairing and angry | Corpses everywhere → pupils shrink → body trembles → heart-tearing roar |

### Emotion Transmission Chain
- Emotion transmission = plot progression
- Emotion venting = source of thrill
- Emotion rhythm: Step by step progression, the perceived prestige gets higher and higher.

---

## XXXIII. Combat Scenes

### Core Principle
**Combat must have meaning, not just fighting for the sake of fighting.**

| ❌ Meaningless Combat | ✅ Meaningful Combat |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Villain brainlessly mocks and gets face-slapped, no follow-up | Transmit emotion: Gratitude/Shock at talent/Introduce new plot |

### Combat Purpose = Transmitting Several Emotions
- **Transmit 1 emotion:** Resolved in one move.
- **Transmit 2 emotions:** Needs to show talent (leapfrog challenge).
- **Transmit 3 emotions:** Needs to show ultimate skills.

### Good Combat Example
Life-and-death duels use suppression before raising: Opponent keeps upper hand → trump card explodes → emotion completely explodes.

## XXXIV. Instance (Dungeon/Arc) Design

### Instance Definition
- Instance = A small segment of story nested in the main story.
- Has a driving effect on the main line.
- Is not a side quest; is a complete event.

### Basic Three-Stage Format
| Stage | Content | Thrill Type |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Origin** | Buildup and anticipation, event preview | Thrill pre-heating |
| **Process** | Plot unfolding, immersion, thrill detonation | Thrill explosion |
| **Result** | Harvest and growth | Thrill aftertaste |

### Design Flow: Reverse Deduction Method
1. **Determine the thrill point first** (Climax/Ending).
2. **Anchor the ending** and deduce the process backward.
3. **Advantage:** Character lines converge, motive and direction are clear.

### Chain-Style Buildup
**❌ Parallel:** A→B→C→D
**✅ Serial (Chained):** A1-A2-B1-A3-B2-C1-B3...

The beginning of the next event is embedded into the process of the previous event.

### Burying Foreshadowing
- **Short-term foreshadowing:** Detonated directly, short and snappy.
- **Long-term foreshadowing:** Buried deep, wait for the ending to detonate.
- After anchoring the ending, all foreshadowing points to the same ending.

### Process Stage
- **Pacing:** Climax amplitude doesn't need to be too large, constantly write small ups and downs.
- **Overly tense** → Reading fatigue.
- **Protagonist's shining point:** Strategy to break the deadlock; having what others don't.

### Result Stage
**Harvest must be useful for subsequent events:**
- Wealth, items, abilities.
- Intel clues, new character relationships.
- Becoming the hook for the next event.

### Pitfalls
- Digging holes is fun, filling them is a nightmare.
- Don't over-pursue rationality; moderation is key.
- Too many big climaxes → exhaustion of anticipation → dropping the book.
- Daily life cannot be filler; must show character's attitude toward something.

## XXXV. Solving Writer's Block

### Three Types of Writer's Block

| Type | Reason | Solution |
|------|--------|----------|
| Plot Stuck | Don't know how to develop next | Review outline → make detailed outline → find supporting lines → change maps |
| No Framework | Started writing without an outline | Organize detailed outline to find unfilled holes |
| Personal Reasons | Emotion/body/inspiration/materials | Rest 1-2 days, look through materials for inspiration |

### Plot Stuck Four-Step Method
1. **Review outline and detailed outline,** check for omissions.
2. **Speed up progress,** streamline the plot, jump out of the stuck area (❌ never use filler).
3. **Change maps,** change branch developments.
4. **Start from supporting characters,** flesh out the plot.

### Pitfalls
- When stuck, **do not write filler to pad word count.**
- The more stuck you are, the more you should streamline the plot; the faster the better.

## XXXVI. Breaking 100,000 Words

### Core Problem
100k words tests comprehensive ability:
- Story framework building
- Main line / branch design
- Character relationship tug-of-war
- Material accumulation

### Think Clearly Before Starting a Book
1. **Is the opening exciting:** Give the punchline, don't unpack it yet.
2. **Is the story framework massive enough:** Can the dilemma/goal/reversals support 500k words?
3. **Are character relationships exciting enough:** Love and hate intertwined, characters people love and hate.
4. **Open and hidden line arrangements:** When to pull out branch lines, how multiple lines run parallel.

### Sources of Material
- Stories of people around you
- Personal experiences of love, hate, success, and failure
- **Art originates from life**

### Pitfalls
- Material accumulation is slow work; accumulate 10 ideas a day.
- A newcomer's first three books are practice; each one gets better.

## XXXVII. Contracting Key Points

### What Editors Look At
1. **Correct Values:** Conforms to mainstream values, no plagiarism.
2. **Reading Experience:** No typos, fluent sentences, clear layout.
3. **Genre Suitability:** Study Fanqie charts and data.
4. **Innovativeness:** Originality > Homogenization.
5. **Anticipation:** Story pacing has proper detail and brevity, thrill points are exciting.

### Pitfalls
- Unlikable persona = toxic point.
- Unsmooth story logic = toxic point.
- Homogenization = hard to get contracted.

## XXXVIII. CP (Couple) Shaping

### Treat the CP as a Whole
Individual personas require inventing conflicts; overall design makes the follow-up simple.

### CP Type × CP Relationship
| CP Type | CP Relationship |
|---------|-----------------|
| Love at first sight / Love over time / Hate turns to love / Step-by-step scheming | One-sided crush / Mutual crush / Open love / Forced CP |

### Natural Conflict + Fatal Attraction
- **Conflict:** Values/stances/class/ethics/life experiences.
- **Attraction:** Can't just have conflicts without sweetness (candy).

### Ways to Add Conflict
- **Value Conflict:** Environmentalist female lead vs. ruthless businessman male lead.
- **Class Conflict:** Heiress vs. street skewer vendor male lead.
- **Ethical Conflict:** Ex-boyfriend's young uncle / pseudo-incest.
- **Identity Conflict:** Monster hunter vs. Monster / Thief vs. Police.

### Character Tags
- **One core tag:** Stands out more than multiple tags.
- Repeatedly use different plots to reinforce this tag.

---

## XXXIX. Avoiding Persona Collapse

### Root Cause
Readers thinking a character reacts inconsistently to something = persona collapse.

### Method 1: Frame the Underlying Logic of Behavior
- Design the deepest behavioral logic for the character.
- Follow this logic throughout the entire text.
- Ex: A noble lady uses rational thinking to treat an irrational world.
  - Facing provocation → handles it indirectly, doesn't fight violence with violence.

### Method 2: Don't Define Characters Absolutely
- Don't separate absolute good and evil, black and white.
- As long as motives and desires don't violate underlying logic.
- Other choices and behaviors are reasonable.

### Method 3: Emphasize Stance and Choices
- **Not opposition of good and evil, but conflict of stances.**
- Ex: Two women fighting for company power.
  - At work: Fighting each other.
  - Outside work: Can help each other.
- Characters can break away from being tools.

### Pitfalls
- Vicious female supporting character doing all bad things = tool person = unlikable.
- A character having only one desire = thin.
- Villain purely causing trouble for the protagonist = unconvincing.

## XL. Composition of Character Bios

### Five Elements
| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Identity Background | Name/Age/Origin/Profession/Faction/Level/Skills/Weapons/Social relations/Appearance |
| Personality Habits | Condensed tags: straightforward/enduring/sinister/cowardly |
| Values & Attitude | Values + attitude toward life, determining reactions to events |
| Ideal Goal | Character's inner drive, representing the story's main line |
| External Pressure | External forces compelling them to do something, clashing with ideals to create story |

### Relationship Between Personality and Background
- Mei Changsu dormant and enduring → framed and narrowly escaped death.
- Zhen Huan likes reading → daughter of a civil official, good family education.
- An Lingrong sinister and ruthless → bullied since childhood, surviving in chaos.

### Habits as Foreshadowing Anchors
- Deep schemer: Doesn't show emotion, plays with a folding fan.
- Straightforward person: Eats heartily, gnaws on chicken legs bare-handed.
- Suspicious person: Lets others eat first.

## XLI. Combined Contrast Tag Method

### Core Formulas
- **"Looks like this, but actually is not"**: Looks weak, actually can lift a tripod.
- **"Appearance is this, but actually is that"**: Looks oily and treacherous, actually innocent and silly.

### Flat vs. Round Characters
| Type | Characteristics |
|------|-----------------|
| Flat Characters | Typified, can be made vivid through contrast tags |
| Round Characters | Complex and multi-faceted, has indelible transformations and growth |

### Peeling Tags Layer by Layer
1. Reveal surface tags first (Appearance/Personality).
2. Apply layers of pressure, forcing choices through conflicts.
3. Finally reveal the deep core (Values attitude/Ideal goals).

### Extremization Principle
- Extreme persona tag = only one corresponding reaction.
- Example: A moderate, rule-abiding person → forced to stand out, feels terrified and anxious, eager to erase it.

## XLII. Protagonist Party Combination

### Number Setting
- Female-oriented: 3 people is best.
- Long novels 1 million+ words: Can be relaxed to 5-7 people.
- Each instance: 1-3 people as core pushers, the rest faded out.

### Introduction Method
- Use events to stick tags on characters.
- Event = driven by external pressure or internal ideal.
- Character behavior motive = revealing persona tags.

### Protagonist Party Goal Setting
- **Common long-term goal:** To pull the long-term plot along.
- Example: Finding five spirit orbs / building a homeland / solving a case to find the truth.

### Intra-Party Persona Requirements
- Must have contrasts with each other.
- Starting points are inconsistent, ending point is consistent = more friction and sparks.
- Do not have two people completely overlap in goals/personality/background.

## XLIII. Character Arc

### Definition
A character making choices amidst external pressures and turning point events, completing a metamorphosis or drastic change in nature.

### Construction Method
1. Ensure the initial tag is clear.
2. Constantly apply pressure, making the character make choices in events.
3. Key event → irreversible turning point → complete tag transformation.

### Arc Examples
| Beginning | Ending |
|-----------|--------|
| "I will sweep away all injustices in the world" | "I stopped practicing sword long ago; the world is like this, I alone cannot change it" |
| "Cleanse the world? Not interested" | "This millennium-old order, I shall challenge it alone" |
| Making generic drugs to make money | Doing it to save lives |

### Protagonist Party Ensemble Arc
- Doesn't need the same time / same event.
- Each has their own main instances, each has their own growth.
- Each instance has one of them as the core action.

## XLIV. Essence of Immersion (Substitution)

### Reader Feelings
- The protagonist is me.
- What "matter" am "I" experiencing.

### Core
- Story core + key resonance.
- Readers can't resonate with the character → can't immerse.

## XLV. Six Pillars of Immersion

### 1. Basic Information Delivery and Tagging
- Let readers recognize the protagonist: appearance/clothing/personality/features.
- Summarized in about a hundred words.
- Example: "I am Lin Feng, heir of the Zhennan Mansion..." → One sentence shows location/person/trait/identity.

### 2. Concretization, Visualization, Familiarity
- Descriptions must be specific to things readers can imagine.
- Readers will associate automatically.
- **Familiarity:** Scenes readers have experienced → natural immersion.
  - Ex: "Breakup after college entrance exam" in the grove behind the teaching building.

### 3. Resonance (Cognitive + Emotional)
**Cognitive Resonance:**
- Protagonist's choices overlap with reader's choices.
- "I would do that too! XX should do exactly this!"

**Emotional Resonance:**
- Use family affection as a bond (easiest).
- Cut in with the weaker party / angry emotions.
- Ex:
  - The grandmother who raised them by collecting scrap is bullied by thugs.
  - War God's daughter living in a doghouse.
  - Sister hospitalized and wages are unfairly withheld.

### 4. Desire and Curiosity
**Basic Desires** (Passive):
- Getting something for nothing, superiority, power and status.
- Readers release emotions through the novel.

**Active Desires** (Anticipation):
- "Emotional gaps" deliberately shaped by the author.
- Ex: "Villain comes to do evil, little knowing the protagonist is no longer the same" → anticipation of face-slapping.

### 5. Five Senses Immersion
Visual/Auditory/Olfactory/Tactile/Gustatory
- Wet short sleeves sticking to the back.
- Smell of disinfectant in the hospital.
- Making readers build the scene in their minds.

### 6. Persona and Immersion
- Imperfect personas are more approachable.
- Characters have flaws → room for growth → character arc.
- Example: Perfect female character's real name is "Niu Cuihua" / Domineering CEO goes home to eat cheap starch sausages.

## XLVI. Stabilizing Plot Pacing

### Genre Selection
Choose genres based on reader needs:
- Author = Salesperson
- Work = Product
- Streets with high foot traffic = Large category genres with many readers

### Attracting Readers in the Opening
**Put the best-looking fruit on the top layer**
- The opening must attract reader curiosity.
- Not "slow burn, gets good later" (equals putting rotten apples on top).

### Step-by-Step Sales Pitch
**Layered Emotional Value:**
- Display fruit → hang a sign "Freshly picked."
- Add a line "The boss ran away with his sister-in-law" → It blows up.

### Plot Hooks
- Leave a hook in the opening to make readers read chapter 2.
- When the hook ends, leave 1-2 more hooks.
- A book = stringing together one hook after another.

### Pacing Example
```
Apples on special for $1 → Buy 10 pounds and qualify to buy bananas → Get a membership card for $50 get $20 free → Join the group chat for flash sales
```

### Plot Progression Pacing
**Press → Relieve → Press Again → Explode:**
1. Press first (oppressive living environment shaping).
2. Relieve (hope/daily life/warmth).
3. Press again (deeper despair).
4. Explode (justice served).

### Methods to Control Pacing
- After outlining, forcibly control the word count.
- If 4000 words are meant to explain something → write exactly that much.
- If written over limit → check if tedious/invalid plot → trim.

---

## XLVII. Million-Word Long Novel Pacing

### Newcomer Dilemmas
- Early thrill points too dense → Mind exhausted after 300-500k words.
- Readers face continuous high pacing → Reading fatigue.
- Discrepancy between early and late pacing is too large → Dropping the book.

**Suggestion: Steady pacing; better to sacrifice early high-density explosions.**

### Pacing for the First 300,000 Words
| Stage | Content | Word Count |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Chap 1-3 | New entry point for immersion | - |
| Chap 4-5 | Throw out settings | - |
| 30k words | Short-term goal explosion + leave foreshadowing | - |
| 30k-100k | Refine persona + worldview | 70k words |
| 100k words | Big explosion | - |
| 100k-300k | Slow down pacing (not too slow) | 200k words |

### Core Method
**Planting mindset:**
- Don't scatter all seeds in one breath.
- Sow by season and variety.
- Always maintain: There is a hole ahead, a shovel in hand, can dig anytime, can fill anytime.

### Daily Small Techniques
- Every 1-2 chapters have attractions.
- Every 30k-50k words have small plots to mobilize emotions.
- Add interesting characters when slowing the pace.

### Pitfalls: Don't Give Readers Multiple-Choice Questions
| ❌ Multiple Choice | ✅ True/False (Judgment) |
|-------------------|--------------------------|
| "To kill or not to kill?" | "A is guilty beyond forgiveness, deserves death" |
| Forcing readers to choose → Diverts traffic, drops book | Right and wrong are clear → No loss of readers |

**Example**:
- Villain slaughters whole family → Is a lunatic, deserves death.
- Male and female leads love each other → Don't make it a "killing father's enemy" multiple-choice.

## XLVIII. Four Ways to Enhance Immersion

| Way | Explanation |
|-----|-------------|
| Familiarity | Familiar items/catchphrases/scenes |
| Tags | Persona tag + small flaw + memory point (birthmark/catchphrase/limp) |
| Conflict Urgency | Crisis sense maxed out, readers are pushed forward |
| Groundedness | Character flaws + colloquial descriptions |

### Prose Pitfalls
- Reduce "le" particles and transition words.
- Overusing "Although... but," "However," "Yet" → forcefully making readers agree with the author.

## XLIX. Persona Three-Dimensional Model

### Protagonist vs. Supporting vs. Villain
| Character | Positioning | Core |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Protagonist | Functionality > Perfection | Has cheat/info gap or abilities others can't do |
| Supporting | Memory Point > Completeness | One unique tag (sweet-toothed shadow guard / stuttering poison doctor) |
| Villain | Destructive Power > Evil Value | Strong power, gives protagonist real pressure |

### Basic Character Model
- Name/Age/Identity (Dual-sided setting)
- Appearance: **Dynamic description** > Static description
- Memory Anchor: One unique feature (scar/catchphrase)

### Background Setting
1. **Family Background:** Root of personality, a cold person hasn't felt love since childhood.
2. **Emotional Ties:** Family/Friendship/Love support the plot.
3. **Dual Identity:** Hidden identity = anticipation (waiting for readers to anticipate the unmasking).

## L. Stereoscopic Personalities (Fleshing out)

### Core Conflict Points (Three Types)
1. **Value Conflict:** New identity vs. old concepts contradiction.
2. **Behavioral Pattern Conflict:** Socially anxious person forced to be a leader.
3. **Cognitive Conflict:** Love-deprived person doesn't believe in being loved.

### Specific Behavioral Reactions
- **Instinctive Reaction:** Immediately drawing sword when betrayed (reckless type).
- **Rational Reaction:** Forcibly suppressing anger to analyze pros and cons (calm type).
- **Hidden Reaction:** Fingertips digging into palms drawing blood (enduring type).

### Language and Action
Words and deeds must match identity, unless there's a hidden identity (can use behavior to expose and lay foreshadowing).

---

## LI. Growth Planning

### Visualization of Growth
Let readers see the protagonist's growth:
- Cultivation level materializing (Spiritual Qi mist → Golden Core solidifying).
- Item upgrade (Electric bike → Supercar keys).
- Clothing changes (Canvas shoes → Suit and leather shoes).

### Blackening/Awakening Trigger Points
3 Key events + 1 Hidden switch:
- Betrayal by friends and family / Power backlash / Collapse of faith
- Unsealing childhood memories / Bloodline curse

## LII. Social Network

### Two Types of Relationships
1. **Family/Blood Relations:** Naturally brings trouble or help (Father is a gambler → child chased for debt).
2. **Strong Persona Ties:** Male and female leads must be tightly bound.

### Three Ways to Enhance Strong Ties
- **Interest Binding:** Holding resources the other needs.
- **Information Gap Control:** Knowing the other's weakness.
- **Emotional Debt:** One party's mistake once caused the other's family to be wronged.

---

## LIII. Strengthening Memory Points

### Signature Behaviors
- Linguistic features: Specific scene lines.
- Action tags: Small actions (always dropping pen when spinning / gnawing clothes when nervous).
- Time tags: Losing all power every full moon (mentioning moon = nervous emotion).

### Contrast Settings
- **Visual Contrast:** Petite girl wielding a giant iron hammer.
- **Behavioral Contrast:** Aloof persona having a weird sense of humor.
- **Linguistic Contrast:** Tough guy's catchphrase is "You're so annoying~"

---

## LIV. Persona Taboos

### Three 'Do Nots' Principle
1. **Supporting characters are independent of protagonist's needs:** Supporting characters have their own emotional demands and dilemmas.
2. **Avoid information dumping:** Use events/profiling instead of directly stating "stubborn personality."
3. **Avoid perfect personas:** Imperfection allows the story to unfold (trash flow / yandere).
