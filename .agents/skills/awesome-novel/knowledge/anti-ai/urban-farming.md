# Urban Farming Anti-AI Rules

> Applicable genres: urban-farming

## High-Frequency AI Flawed Sentences: Good vs. Bad Examples

### 1. Harvest Descriptions

❌ "Golden waves of wheat undulated with the wind, a scene of bumper harvest."
✅ "He bundled the wheat and stacked it on the ridge of the field. The wheat awns pricked his hands, so he shrank them into his sleeves. The sun was about to set; there were still two rows left."

### 2. Direct Statement of Emotion

❌ "He felt life was full of hope."
✅ "He flipped a page on the calendar. Three months left. Three months is enough to plant another crop. He hung the calendar back up; the nail was crooked, so be it."

❌ "She felt sweet and happy in her heart."
✅ "She washed the vegetables three times. The water was cold, and her fingertips were a bit red. She started humming a song, couldn't find the tune, but sang it anyway."

### 3. Parallel Structure Expectation

❌ "Perhaps tomorrow, perhaps the day after tomorrow, perhaps the harvest will come soon."
✅ "He squatted in the shed looking at the melons. The melons weren't ripe yet, about the size of a fist. He pinched one with his fingernail, but it wouldn't yield. Wait a bit longer."

### 4. Overly Beautified Descriptions

❌ "A pastoral life like a paradise, with birds singing and flowers fragrant."
✅ "The chickens were clucking noisily, and the smell of manure drifted over. He pinched his nose and went to check the pen, then came back and washed his hands three times."

### 5. Direct Statement of Achievement

❌ "He finally set foot on the road to wealth relying on his hardworking hands."
✅ "He counted the money. Then counted it again. After counting, he tied it up with a rubber band and stuffed it into a tin box. The tin box was a bit rusty, and the lid was hard to open."

## Writing Key Points

1. **Write farming as exhausting** — Sore back, callused hands, sunburns, not "the joy of harvest."
2. **Write hope through small actions** — Calendars, seed bags, tin boxes; concrete items of accumulation are more real than "hope."
3. **Write daily life as trivial** — Washing vegetables three times, watering a few scoops, bending over to weed, not "hardworking cultivation."
4. **Write achievement as counting/saving money** — Money is the most concrete embodiment of achievement for rural people.
5. **Pace leans slow** — Farming novels are not fast-paced; let details accumulate slowly.
