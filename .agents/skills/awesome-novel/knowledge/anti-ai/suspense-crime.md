# Suspense / Crime Investigation Anti-AI Rules

> Applicable genres: suspense-crime

## High-Frequency AI Flawed Sentences: Good vs. Bad Examples

### 1. Clue Discovery

❌ "He noticed a fountain pen on the table; this detail caught his attention."
✅ "There was a fountain pen on the table. He picked it up and looked; the ink was new. It had been used, but hadn't been refilled recently."

### 2. Direct Statement of Reasoning

❌ "He deduced that the killer must be an insider."
✅ "He stared at that list. Circled three people. Turned the paper over; the back was blank."

❌ "He seemed to have thought of something."
✅ "He paused. Tapped his finger on the table twice. Then pushed his chair back a bit."

### 3. Parallel Structure Clue Analysis

❌ "Perhaps it was a crime of passion, perhaps for money, or perhaps for revenge."
✅ "He smoked cigarette after cigarette. The ashtray was full. There were also a few crumpled paper balls on the floor."

### 4. Direct Statement of Emotion

❌ "He felt the case becoming increasingly confusing and complicated."
✅ "He rubbed his eyes. The words on the paper started to sway. Looked at it all night, didn't figure anything out."

### 5. Truth Revelation

❌ "It turned out that everything was under his control."
✅ "He closed the folder. The edges of the binder were frayed; it had been used for a long time. He leaned back in his chair and sighed."

## Writing Key Points

1. **Write clues through details** — Don't write "noticed," write "the pen is new," "the ashtray is full."
2. **Write reasoning through actions** — Rubbing eyes, tapping the table, looking at the same piece of paper over and over.
3. **Write emotions through states** — Exhaustion, anxiety, being stuck; write specific physical reactions.
4. **End truth revelation with an image** — Don't write "so that's how it was," end with a meaningful visual image.
5. **Keep the pace oppressive** — Suspense shouldn't always be fast; appropriately slow down to write about waiting and torment.
