# Historical Ancient Anti-AI Rules

> Applicable genres: historical, historical-brained, ancient-politics

## High-Frequency AI Flawed Sentences: Good vs. Bad Examples

### 1. Piling up Environment Descriptions

❌ "In the main hall with its carved beams and painted rafters, resplendent and magnificent, the atmosphere was solemn and majestic."
✅ "The pillars in the main hall were thick, taking two people to embrace. The floor was made of green bricks, polished by footsteps until it shone. He counted; there were six bricks on the floor that were lighter in color, having been patched."

### 2. Direct Statement of Emotion

❌ "A complex emotion surged in his heart."
✅ "He lowered his head, his fingers clenching inside his sleeves, nails digging into his palms. A smile still hung on his face, not even lifting his eyelids."

❌ "She was startled in her heart."
✅ "Her hand paused. The needle stuck in the cloth, unmoving. She paused for two breaths before continuing to sew."

### 3. Parallel Structure Psychology

❌ "Perhaps he saw it wrong, perhaps he was making a fuss over nothing, or perhaps he was just too tired these past few days."
✅ "He stared at that line of words, reading it three times. By the fourth time, a bird chirped outside the window; he looked up, then looked back down, and the words were still standing there."

### 4. Textbook Action Descriptions

❌ "He cupped his hands and bowed, respectfully retreating outside."
✅ "He bent his waist, the angle neither too large nor too small, just enough for courtesy. He took three steps backward, then turned around. The door threshold was a bit high; as he stepped over, his sleeve brushed against it."

### 5. Overly Literary Dialogue

❌ "What Your Excellency says is absolutely right, this humble official is suddenly enlightened."
✅ "He nodded. Clumsy with words, he couldn't say anything pleasant to hear. 'Yes.' Just one word. Head lowered, the toe of his shoe scraping against the ground."

## Writing Key Points

1. **Write architecture through details** — How thick are the pillars? How many floor bricks are patched? How high is the threshold?
2. **Write etiquette through actions** — Cupping hands, bowing, kneeling; use specific actions to gloss over them, do not expand on the description.
3. **Use the face and hands for emotions** — Ancient people suppressed their emotions, relying entirely on small actions to leak them.
4. **Dialogue should be half-literary, half-colloquial** — Don't use overly literary catchphrases, but also avoid overly modern plain speech.
5. **Write hierarchy through details** — What they eat, what they wear, where they sit; these details are more effective than directly stating "noble status."
