# Complete Guide to Removing AI Flavor

> Methodological basis for all Phases of anti-ai. Covers AI writing fingerprint identification, systematic modification methods, and rewriting examples.

---

## I. AI Writing Fingerprints

AI-generated texts have stable pattern features on a statistical level. The following are the basis for fingerprint identification.

### 1.1 High-Frequency Word Fingerprints

AI prefers to use certain high-frequency words, which are sparsely distributed in human writing:

| Category | Typical Words |
|----------|---------------|
| Modal Adverbs | suddenly, as if, couldn't help but, still, gradually |
| Weakened Judgments | seemingly, perhaps, probably, somewhat, a certain kind |
| Formal Connectors | however, at the same time, thus it can be seen, to sum up |
| Universal Verbs | reveal, fall into, surge, well up, flash past |
| Psychological Guidance | feel, think, realize, understand |

### 1.2 Chapter-End Summary Style

AI has a strong tendency to summarize and elevate the theme at the end of a chapter:

- "He didn't know what he was about to face..."
- "From this moment on, everything will be different"
- "This night, is destined to..."
- "Perhaps, this is the meaning of growth"

### 1.3 Stacked Descriptions

AI tends to use multi-layered stacked modifiers to create a "literary feel":

- Multi-layered attributes: "A complex, indescribable emotion flashed through his eyes, one that even he couldn't explain clearly."
- Emotion + Action + Environment combo: "He took a deep breath, looking at the distant skyline, his heart filled with mixed feelings."
- Stacked adverbs: "He seemed to vaguely feel..."

### 1.4 Uniform Distribution

On a micro level, AI has a stable tendency towards uniform distribution:

- **Uniform sentence length**: Sentence lengths fluctuate very little; extremely short 2-3 word sentences rarely appear.
- **Uniform paragraph length**: Paragraph lengths are centrally distributed, lacking very short paragraphs (1-2 sentences) and very long paragraphs (10+ sentences).
- **Uniform word frequency**: The distribution of word frequencies is flat, lacking the reasonable repetition of core vocabulary.
- **Uniform punctuation**: The ratio of periods to commas is stable, lacking unconventional punctuation like em dashes, enumeration commas, etc.

## II. Core Rules

### Rule 1: Paragraph Density

| Principle | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| One paragraph, one event | Do not cram actions, environment, dialogue, and emotions into the same paragraph. |
| Decreasing information density | Key information first, supplementary information later. |
| Short paragraphs preferred | If you can say it in 2 sentences, don't use 5. |

Ideal paragraph length distribution (measured in sentences, qualitative guidance, not strict metrics):

| Paragraph Type | Suggested Ratio | Function |
|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| Single-sentence paragraph | 10%-30% | Rhythm change, emphasis (do not use single-sentence paragraphs throughout the whole chapter) |
| 2-5 sentence paragraph | Main narrative | Regular paragraphs, moderate information density |
| 6+ sentence paragraph | Use as needed | Allowed for complex scenes, careful not to make information density too uniform |

### Rule 2: Action + Dialogue + Emotion

Priority of emotion description:

1. **Action** (Best): "He slammed the cup onto the table."
2. **Dialogue** (Second best): "What do you mean?"
3. **Physical reaction** (Third): "His hands were shaking."
4. **Direct statement** (Worst, only allowed as brief 1-2 sentence statements at critical emotional nodes): "He was very angry."

### Rule 3: Combining Long and Short Sentences

- Long and short sentences should alternate naturally; avoid all long sentences or all short sentences.
- Key information can be highlighted with short sentences; atmosphere description can be laid out with long sentences.
- Long sentences (>40 words) are suggested to be separated by short sentences to adjust the rhythm.

### Rule 4: Colloquialism

- Dialogue should be short, fragmented, and leave things unsaid.
- Narrative language leans towards a colloquial rhythm, not a formal essay.
- Daily colloquial words like "then," "anyway," "actually" are allowed, but at low frequency.

## III. Show Don't Tell Principle

### 3.1 Core Formula

```
Tell: Emotion words / Judgment sentences → The reader is told
Show: Physical reactions / Actions / Environment / Dialogue → The reader feels it themselves
```

### 3.2 Comparison Table

| Tell (Prohibited) | Show (Recommended) |
|-------------------|--------------------|
| He was very nervous | He tapped the edge of the table three times, then three times more. |
| She was very angry | She slammed the phone screen-down on the table. |
| It was a dilapidated small town | Where the wall plaster peeled, red bricks were exposed, and bedsheets hung in the alleys. |
| He felt uneasy | Every two steps he took, he looked back once. |
| She was very happy | She tapped her toes on the ground twice. |
| The atmosphere was awkward | He looked down and drank water, and when he finished, he found her still looking at him. |
| His mind was a mess | He lit a cigarette, took a drag, and put it out. |

### 3.3 Five Senses Check

For every scene description, ask yourself:

- [ ] Visual — What did he see? (Not "beautiful," but "there are cracks in the wall")
- [ ] Auditory — What did he hear? (Not "quiet," but "only the clock is ticking")
- [ ] Tactile — What did he feel? (Not "cold," but "the door handle felt icy")
- [ ] Olfactory — What did he smell? (Not "fragrant," but "the medicinal soup was so bitter it wrinkled his entire face")
- [ ] Kinesthetic — What was his body doing? (Not "standing," but "he shifted his weight to the other foot")

## IV. Detection of 7 AI Writing Patterns

### Pattern 1: Summary Openings/Endings

```
Signal: Summary judgments appear at the beginning or end of a paragraph.
Features: "This is," "This means," "He understood," "In general"
Fix: Delete the summary, stop at a specific image/action.
```

### Pattern 2: Psychological Analysis Reports

```
Signal: Large blocks of structured psychological descriptions (>3 continuous psychological sentences).
Features: "Deep in his heart he knew...", "On the one hand... on the other hand..."
Fix: Break them into 1-2 sentences; externalize the rest with actions.
```

### Pattern 3: Perfect Logic Chains

```
Signal: Every sentence is the direct causal result of the previous one.
Features: No idle writing, no zoning out, no irrelevant details.
Fix: Insert an irrelevant observation between two causal sentences.
```

### Pattern 4: Uniform Information Density

```
Signal: Every paragraph pushes the plot forward; there are no variations in density.
Features: Information concentration is consistent from start to finish, without breathing room paragraphs.
Fix: Choose a paragraph to insert slice-of-life details (zoning out/looking out the window/thinking of unrelated things).
```

### Pattern 5: Template Emotional Reactions

```
Signal: Emotional reactions are the "most reasonable choices."
Features: Crying when one should cry, angry when one should be angry, nervous when one should be nervous; completely impeccable throughout.
Fix: Change one instance to a mismatched reaction—laughing when one should cry, zoning out when one should be angry, getting off-topic when one should be nervous.
```

### Pattern 6: Completely Smooth Transitions

```
Signal: All paragraph transitions are smooth and natural.
Features: No abrupt jumps; every paragraph starts with a smooth continuation.
Fix: If all paragraph transitions in a chapter are smooth with no variation, consider deleting 1-2 transition sentences to create a rhythm change.
Note: Do not make hard cuts just for the sake of it—natural transitions are necessary.
```

### Pattern 7: Uniform Punctuation

```
Signal: The ratio of periods and commas is stable, lacking unconventional punctuation.
Features: Almost no use of em dashes, enumeration commas, ellipses; little variation in sentence length.
Fix: Add 1-2 fragmented sentences using enumeration commas/ellipses to create a sense of punctuation irregularity.
```

### Pattern 8: Symmetrical Padding

```
Signal: Sentence structures are symmetrical and neat, with equal weight before and after.
Features: "Not only X but also Y," "Both X and Y," "From A to B"
Fix: Break the symmetry, keep only the stronger side, delete the weaker side directly or change it to an independent short sentence.
      Not "both nervous and excited" → "He was so nervous his palms sweated. And a little excited."
      Not "from morning to dusk" → "It got light. And then dark."
```

### Pattern 9: Mechanical Enumeration

```
Signal: Explicit sequential or parallel structures, reading like an outline.
Features: "First... second... last," "Number one... number two... number three," "On one hand... on the other hand..."
Fix: Break it down into natural narration; replace sequential words with time/causality/feelings.
```

### Pattern 10: Rhetorical Question & Assumption Guides

```
Signal: Throwing out a rhetorical question/assumption first before answering it, creating unnecessary suspense.
Features: "If I told you...", "Have you ever thought...", "Imagine..."
Fix: Delete the guiding sentence and state it directly. Readers don't need to be asked before being told.
```

### Pattern 11: Positive Energy Wrap-ups

```
Signal: Chicken soup/motivational/hopeful summaries appear at the end of a chapter.
Features: "The future is promising," "Let's wait and see," "There are long days ahead" (including variants).
Fix: Delete, stop at a specific image/action. Leave the emotion for the reader to digest on their own.
```

### Pattern 12: Unsourced Citations

```
Signal: Mentioning research/data/evidence without providing a source.
Features: "Research shows," "According to statistics," "Surveys indicate"
Fix: Delete "research shows" or add a specific source. In fiction, the vast majority can be directly deleted.
```

## V. Systematic De-AI Three-Pass Method

### Pass 1: De-generalization (~80% of AI flavor is concentrated here)

**Goal**: Replace abstract/cliché expressions with concrete details.

Steps:
1. Scan the whole text for forbidden words (★★★★★ most toxic sentence structures → Level 1 forbidden words).
2. Replace modal words (as if / a trace of / couldn't help but → delete or describe plainly).
3. Replace emotion words (angry / sad → physical reactions).
4. Replace template descriptions (a flash of light in the eyes → action).
5. Replace sentence structures (not A but B → write B directly).

Validation: Forbidden word hit rate drops by over 80%.

### Pass 2: De-formalization

**Goal**: Formal tone / translated tone → colloquialisms and actions.

Steps:
1. Scan for omniscient narration (He didn't know... → delete).
2. Scan for psychological analysis (Deep in his heart → externalize as action).
3. Remove internal debate matches (A voice said... → keep the conclusion).
4. Simplify dialogue tags (He said angrily → action continuation).
5. Remove adverbial modifiers (He slowly → delete).

Validation: Dialogue tag density <10%, no omniscient narration.

### Pass 3: Return to Naturalness

**Goal**: Make the text look like it was written by a human—natural pauses and rhythm changes.

Steps:
1. Check if the rhythm is too uniform—if so, create natural ups and downs (alternating long/short, information density changes).
2. Add idle writing (choose a paragraph to insert slice-of-life details that do not serve the main plot).
3. Add misalignment (suggest at least 1 emotional contrast or abnormal reaction).
4. Fix endings (elevation/summary/preview → image/action).

Validation: Paragraph lengths have natural variation, at least 1 instance of idle writing, at least 1 emotional contrast. Avoid deliberately fragmenting sentences.

## VI. Rewriting Examples Library

### 6.1 Externalizing Emotions

**Anger:**

❌ "A wave of uncontrollable rage surged in his heart."

✅ "He slammed the cup on the table. Water splashed out and ran down the edge. He didn't wipe it."

**Nervousness:**

❌ "He was so nervous his palms were sweating."

✅ "He wiped his hand on his trouser leg. Wiped it again. The edge of the table was rubbed shiny by him."

**Sorrow:**

❌ "She felt a wave of indescribable sorrow."

✅ "She folded the jacket in three and put it in the deepest part of the wardrobe. The zipper wasn't pulled to the end. She unzipped it and zipped it up properly again."

**Surprise:**

❌ "He was so shocked he couldn't speak."

✅ "He opened his mouth. No sound came out. Opened it again, still no sound."

### 6.2 Scene Descriptions

**Environment:**

❌ "As the sun set in the west, the clouds on the horizon were dyed a golden red, breathtakingly beautiful."

✅ "At a quarter past six, the sun hadn't fully set. Shadows were drawn very long; his shadow was two heads taller than himself. The clouds weren't red, they were gray, like unwashed rags."

**Weather:**

❌ "The rain fell heavily, as if there was a hole in the sky."

✅ "The rain beat on the tin roof, so loud you couldn't hear people talk. His trouser legs were soaked, and water had gotten into his shoes."

**Architecture:**

❌ "The castle stood towering, its spires reaching straight into the clouds."

✅ "There were cracks in the castle walls. When he walked past, he counted them; there were seven. The longest one could fit two fingers inside."

### 6.3 Fighting

❌ "His figure flashed, dodging the opponent's attack, and immediately followed up with a spinning kick landing squarely on the opponent's chest."

✅ "The opponent threw a punch. He tilted his head, half a beat too slow, the wind of the punch grazing his ear, leaving it ringing. He didn't do a spinning kick—he wasn't that agile—he crashed forward, his forehead smashing into the opponent's nose."

### 6.4 Endings

**Bad Example:**

❌ "Looking at the distant skyline, his heart was filled with anticipation for the future. What he didn't know was that even greater challenges were waiting for him ahead. This night was destined to be extraordinary."

✅ "He put out the cigarette. Crushed it with the sole of his shoe. Turned around and walked back."

**Bad Example:**

❌ "From this moment on, he finally understood that true courage is not the absence of fear, but pressing forward despite being filled with fear."

✅ "He stood up. Two blades of grass were stuck to his knees. He patted them, but they didn't fall off. Never mind."

### 6.5 Rhythm Comparison

**AI Uniform Rhythm:**

"He walked into the room and looked around; the room was very quiet, only the clock was ticking. He opened the curtains, sunlight shone in, and dust floated in the beam of light. He sat down on the edge of the bed, and the mattress bounced."

**Human Fragmented Rhythm:**

"He pushed the door open. The clock was ticking. No other sound.
The moment the curtains were pulled open, the dust tumbled in the light beam, then sank back down.
He sat down on the bed. It bounced twice."

### 6.6 Physical Reaction Replacements

**Pupils shrank:**

❌ "His pupils shrank slightly."

✅ "He stared for three seconds. Looked down and turned a page of the document. Turned it back and read it again."

**Gasped a breath of cold air:**

❌ "He gasped a breath of cold air."

✅ "He leaned back. The back of the chair dug painfully into his spine. He sat forward again."

**Body stiffened:**

❌ "His body stiffened."

✅ "His feet were nailed to the spot, unmoving. Someone walked around him, but he didn't notice."

**Surged in heart:**

❌ "A wave of warmth surged in his heart."

✅ "He cupped that cup of tea in his hands. Didn't drink. Even when the tea went cold, he didn't put it down."

**Revealed/Showed on face:**

❌ "A smile was revealed on his face."

✅ "He smiled. Halfway through the smile, realizing no one was reciprocating, he took the smile back."

## VII. Quality Check List

### Self-Check After Modification

- [ ] No ★★★★★ most toxic sentence structures.
- [ ] No Level 1 forbidden words.
- [ ] Level 2 forbidden words do not exceed the threshold.
- [ ] Each paragraph focuses on only one event.
- [ ] Paragraph lengths have natural variations; avoid complete uniformity.
- [ ] At least 1 instance of idle writing (zoning out/looking out the window/irrelevant details).
- [ ] At least 1 emotional contrast (abnormal reaction/mismatched reaction).
- [ ] Dialogue tags are not dense (no 3 consecutive sentences with the same tag).
- [ ] Ending stops on an image/action, no elevation, no summary, no preview.
- [ ] No "as if/like/just like" metaphors.
- [ ] Em dashes ≤ 3 times/chapter, no abuse of multiple dashes in a single paragraph.
- [ ] Paragraphs have length variations; no consecutive paragraphs of the same length.
- [ ] Emotions rely on actions/dialogue/physical reactions, not direct psychological statements.
- [ ] Consistent with original plot, no added/deleted plot points.
