# Historical Politics / Ancient Court Intrigue Anti-AI Rules

> Applicable genres: ancient-politics, historical-brained

## High-Frequency AI Flawed Sentences: Good vs. Bad Examples

### 1. Piling up Court Descriptions

❌ "In the Golden Throne Hall, civil and military officials were arrayed on both sides, the atmosphere solemn and majestic."
✅ "It was stuffy in the main hall. The windows were open, but no breeze came in. His back was covered in sweat, sticking to his clothes. The old man next to him coughed, and he shuddered in response."

### 2. Formulaic Intrigue Descriptions

❌ "A glint of light flashed in his eyes, and a strategy was already formed in his mind."
✅ "He set down the teacup. He traced a line on the table with his finger. He didn't speak for a long time. Only after the person next to him urged him twice did he finally open his mouth."

❌ "Everything is under control."
✅ "He burned the envelope. The fire wasn't big, but the black smoke was choking. He squatted there watching it, only standing up when it was completely burned."

### 3. Parallel Structure Analysis

❌ "Perhaps it was the civil officials acting as a constraint, perhaps it was the military generals, or perhaps the emperor didn't trust him."
✅ "He looked at the list. Crossed out three names, added two. The paper was crumpled, then smoothed out, and rewritten."

### 4. Direct Statement of Emotion

❌ "He was secretly overjoyed in his heart, but didn't show it."
✅ "He picked up his wine, brought it to his lips, but didn't drink. The corner of his mouth twitched, then quickly tightened. His eyes were still fixed on the hall doors."

### 5. Overly Literary Dialogue

❌ "What my beloved minister says is very true, We are greatly comforted."
✅ "He nodded. 'Got it.' Just two words. No one below dared to look up."

## Writing Key Points

1. **Write the court as stuffy/humid** — People involved in intrigue are tense; physical discomfort is more realistic than psychological tension.
2. **Write strategies as actions** — Not "a strategy was already formed in his mind," but scratching paper, crumpling paper, burning paper.
3. **Use restraint for emotions** — Neither joy nor anger should be overt; use the corners of the mouth, fingers, and eyes.
4. **Dialogue must be short** — People in court intrigue hold back thirty percent of what they want to say; they touch on the point and stop.
5. **Pass information using objects** — Letters, seals, and memorials carry more weight than saying it directly.
