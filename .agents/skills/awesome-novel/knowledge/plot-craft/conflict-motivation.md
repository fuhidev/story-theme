# Quick Reference for Conflict Motivation Types

> A reference framework used during the volume outline phase to judge whether the "reasons that cannot be compromised" of both parties in a conflict are valid.
> The agent reads and matches this automatically; do not show it to the author.

---

## Valid Motivation Types

The following motivations ensure the character "will lose something tangible if they lose/back down," making the conflict stand firm.

| Type | Essence | Example | Judgment Sign |
|------|---------|---------|---------------|
| **Survival Threat** | Losing means death/family's death/collapse of survival foundation | "If this case is exposed, the silencers will come after my whole family." | Do not back down → Live; Back down → Die/Lose everything |
| **Identity/Self-Worth** | Backing down means no longer being "oneself" | "I am a cop. If I let this case go, I am not human anymore." | Back down = Denying who they are |
| **Obsession/Trauma** | Failed in this before, cannot fail again | "My partner died on this case three years ago; I promised to see it through to the end." | Back down = Betraying the past/betraying the dead |
| **Irreversible Loss** | Backing down means never getting it back; there is no second chance | "She only has 72 hours left; if I back down, she will never be found." | Window of opportunity, countdown, only chance |
| **Emotional Bonding** | Backing down means betraying someone | "He saved my life; I cannot walk away when he is in trouble." | Back down = Defaulting on an emotional debt |
| **Moral Bottom Line** | Backing down means never being able to live with oneself | "I can lose, but I cannot win by framing someone." | Back down = Denying one's own moral code |

### Usage Rules

- At least one side of the conflict must cover **1 kind** of valid motivation.
- The motivations of both sides must be **mutually exclusive** — if A's survival threat clashes with B's identity, neither side can back down.
- The same character can activate different motivation types in different volumes; their foundation remains unchanged, but the situation changes.

## Invalid Motivations (Follow-up Questions Signal)

The following motivations look like reasons but actually cannot justify a "no backing down" behavior. If any appear → Ask the author to provide specific details.

| Type | Example | Why it is invalid | Follow-up Direction |
|------|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Naturally Evil** | "He is just a bad guy" "Villains don't need reasons" | It does not explain why they cannot back down — bad guys can back down too. | "Why did he choose this path? What will he lose if he backs down?" |
| **Plot Necessity** | "He has to stop the protagonist, otherwise the story cannot continue" | Meta reason, not a character's reason. | "From this character's perspective, what personal loss is at stake?" |
| **Abstract Concepts** | "For justice" "To protect world peace" | Too distant and grand, does not land on specific losses. | "What happens if justice is destroyed? What does this loss mean to him personally?" |
| **Professional Obligation** | "He is a cop, so he must stop the protagonist" | Professional requirements allow resignation; they are not non-negotiable. | "Besides professional identity, what personal reason does he have not to back down?" |
| **Following Orders** | "His superiors told him to do it" | Obedience can be interrupted — superiors die/orders are revoked. | "If the order is revoked, would he still want to do it himself?" |

## Mutual Exclusivity Check

It is not enough for both sides to have motivations — the motivations must be irreconcilable. Judge according to the following levels:

```
Irreconcilable (Hard Conflict):
  Protagonist wants X ← Villain wants Y, and X and Y cannot coexist.
  Example: Protagonist wants to expose the truth ← Villain wants to cover it up (If X succeeds, Y inevitably fails).

Reconcilable (Soft Conflict, needs reinforcement):
  Protagonist wants X ← Villain wants Y, but there is a plan Z that allows both sides to compromise.
  Example: Protagonist wants to investigate a case ← Villain wants to stop him → Protagonist can just investigate another case to solve it.

No Conflict (Zero Conflict):
  Protagonist wants X, Villain's goal is unrelated to X.
  Example: Protagonist wants to investigate a case, Villain just wants to watch from the sidelines → This is not a conflict.
```

### Reinforcement Methods

If a conflict falls into the "Reconcilable" level, choose the following methods to reinforce it:

1. **Add a countdown/window of opportunity** — "Can only choose one, no time to do both."
2. **Add irreversible loss** — "If you back down this step, the next chance will never come again."
3. **Add emotional bonding** — "This matter involves someone who cannot be let down."
4. **Add an ethical dilemma** — "Choosing X does not mean avoiding the cost of Y; there are no clean options."
