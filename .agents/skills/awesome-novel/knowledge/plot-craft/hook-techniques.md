# Methodology of Hooks and Suspense

> Three top-tier suspense hook writing techniques, deconstructed from millions of blockbuster works.
> Applicable for hooks design in the chapter outline phase, used in conjunction with the `payoff_plan` in `chapter-setting-style.md`.

---

## 1. Cognitive Dislocation Hook — Counterintuitive Suspense Creation

**Core:** Throw out an outcome that goes against common sense, hide the cause, and create a logical gap. When a "?" pops up in the reader's head, they will follow your pen.

### Execution Rules

- Directly present abnormal phenomena at the beginning, deliberately leaving the cause blank.
- Use the strong contrast of "common sense vs. reality" to create questions.
- Complete the contrast drop within the first 100 words without redundant buildup.

```
❌ The protagonist dies unexpectedly, time-travels back home, and has many complex past grievances.

✅ He resurrects from the dead and steps into his home, his parents looking terrified —
   just a moment ago, the very two people standing before him were the ones who personally ended his life.
```

**Key Point:** Throw the abnormal phenomenon in the reader's face like a bomb, then turn around and run. **Do not explain the setting at the beginning.**

---

## 2. Information Asymmetry Hook — Creating Emotion through Imbalance

### Variation A: Protagonist & Reader Know, Others Don't (Playing the Pig to Eat the Tiger / Rebirth Genre)

**Core:** The reader and protagonist hold advantageous information, while supporting characters/villains are kept in the dark and constantly provoke, amplifying the expectation for a face-slapping twist.

```
❌ The protagonist possesses great power, and everyone quickly discovers it and reveres him.

✅ With the apocalypse approaching, he desperately hoards supplies. Neighbors only think he is greedy for petty gains and acting weirdly,
   constantly mocking him. No one knows he holds the answers to all future crises.
```

**Key Point:** This is expectation-based suspense — toss the bait to the reader in advance, and the reader must endure the preceding buildup to see the satisfying ending.

### Variation B: Reader Knows, Protagonist Doesn't (Oppressive Anxiety)

**Core:** Borrowing the God's-eye view to disclose a crisis to the reader in advance, while the protagonist remains completely unaware. Readers will feel a strong sense of anxiety — "Don't step into it!"

```
❌ A friend harbors evil intentions, preparing to poison and frame the protagonist.

✅ The protagonist drinks heartily, completely trusting his newly made friend.
   No one knows that the other party has just laced the wine with deadly poison and is now hiding behind the door, watching coldly.
```

**Key Point:** Use the reader's God's-eye view to create a sense of participation. The reader is not a spectator, but a participant racing alongside the protagonist.

---

## 3. Rapid Countdown Hook — Time-Limited Crisis

**Core:** Directly bind a countdown to death/punishment/despair to the protagonist right at the start, using human instinct for crisis to retain the reader.

### Execution Rules

- Throw out the time limit + fatal consequence in the very first paragraph.
- Weaken any recap of previous events and directly push the protagonist into a corner.
- Constantly reinforce the oppressive feeling of "time ticking away" throughout.

```
❌ The protagonist is stuck in prison, in a difficult situation, and will be punished later.

✅ He wakes up in the death row with only two days left; if he cannot uncover the truth, he will be exiled to the savage borders.
```

**Key Point:** What the reader is thinking is not "Is this book well written," but "How does he survive."

---

## General Principles

> Do not reveal all your cards on the first page. **You must peel it like an onion; peel one layer, and there is another layer inside.**
> Leave a new question at every ending so that after reading, the reader's mind isn't saying, "Oh, so that's how it is,"
> but rather, "Holy crap, what happens next?"

| Hook Type | Reader Psychology | Best Position to Use |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Cognitive Dislocation | "Why?" — Desire to solve a puzzle | Beginning/End of a chapter |
| Information Asymmetry A (Protagonist knows) | "Slap their faces quickly!" — Expectation | Buildup segments, daily life segments |
| Information Asymmetry B (Reader knows) | "Don't step into it!" — Anxiety | Before a crisis, before a confrontation |
| Rapid Countdown | "Is there enough time?" — Urgency | Beginning, turning points |
