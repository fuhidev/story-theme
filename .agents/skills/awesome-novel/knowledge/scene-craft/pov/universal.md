# POV Switching Techniques

> Abrupt POV (Point of View) switches are major killers of reader retention. Here are three techniques to make perspective transitions natural and smooth, providing both immersion and a premium feel.
>
> This methodology is always loaded—POV handling is involved regardless of the scene type.


## 1. POV Boundary Principle — One Scene, One Core POV

**Core:** Two or more character POVs cannot appear within a single natural paragraph. Stick to one core POV per scene—whoever you start with, stay inside their head the entire time.

### Execution Rules

- If the protagonist can't see it, don't write it.
- If the protagonist can't guess it, write the other party's expression through their observation; do not directly write the other party's inner thoughts.

```
❌ Zhang San thought to himself that this meal was really awful. Li Si looked at Zhang San and thought, why does he look so disgusted.

✅ Zhang San picked up a piece of meat, chewed twice, and frowned.
   Li Si, sitting opposite, noticed him put down his chopsticks, his expression not looking too good.
```

### Handling Mandatory POV Switches: Physical Paragraphing Method

| Method | Usage | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Divider | `···` separation | Explicitly tells the reader the camera has switched |
| Subheading | First half "Ye Chen's Trump Card", second half "Lin Aotian's Despair" | Chapter-level POV cutting |

**Key Point:** Ensure the reader's emotions remain continuous; do not jump back and forth repeatedly between the protagonist and supporting characters.


## 2. Physical Transition — Finding a Physical Trigger for POV Switching

**Core:** The POV cannot just jump over; it must walk over naturally. Use line of sight, sound, or action as the handover point.

### Technique 1: Line of Sight Guidance

Bring the camera to the other party through the protagonist's line of sight.

```
❌ Ye Chen was eating downstairs. At this moment, in a private room on the second floor, Lin Aotian coldly snorted, thinking this Ye Chen was really courting death.

✅ Ye Chen looked up toward the second floor.
   Behind the carved curtains, Lin Aotian stood with his hands behind his back, his gaze meeting Ye Chen's exactly.
   The killing intent in his eyes instantly rose.
```

### Technique 2: Sound Medium

Bring the camera toward the sound source through heard sounds.

```
Downstairs, Ye Chen was drinking wine when he suddenly heard the crisp sound of a glass breaking on the second floor.
He looked up toward the second floor.
   Lin Aotian had smashed his teacup, shards scattering across the floor.
```

### Technique 3: Action Relay

Pass the narrative authority from one person to another using an action.

```
Zhang San stared blankly at the photo on the table.
   A hand rested on his shoulder; looking back, it was Li Si.

   Looking at Zhang San's red-rimmed eyes, Li Si's heart skipped a beat.
```

**Key Point:** Like passing a baton—the intersection point of the action is the handover point of the POV.


## 3. Information Gap Mismatch Method — Switching POVs is for Creating Contrast

**Core:** Switching POVs is not to explain the plot; it is to create information gaps and satisfaction. Both sides writing the same content is nonsense literature.

### Execution Rules

- Switches must be fast—like a flashback or reaction shot in a movie, give a close-up and immediately pull back.
- Supporting character POVs always serve the protagonist—all supporting character POVs exist to elevate the protagonist.
- Do not write two or three thousand words in a supporting character's POV; readers will drop the book because they want to see the protagonist too much.

```
❌ The protagonist carried the furnace and was very happy. Supporting character POV: The protagonist is really happy.

✅ The protagonist successfully refined a supreme-grade pill in public.
   Below the stage, the alchemy master who had always looked down on the protagonist:
   "This is impossible, this technique was clearly lost... How could a piece of trash like him do it?"
```

### Information Gap Switches that Create Suspense

```
The protagonist tells his wife he's going to a dinner party soon.
   The villain is arranging for someone to kill the protagonist at the dinner party.
   The reader knows about the trap; the protagonist does not.
   Suspense: The reader wants to see how the protagonist survives.
```

**Forbidden:** Staying in the supporting character's POV for too long after switching; the content of both POVs being repetitive with no information gap.


## 4. Camera-Style Writing (Camera as Camera)

**Core Principle:** Forget the omniscient point of view; you are a camera following behind the protagonist. Readers can only see what the protagonist sees, hears, smells, and touches—do not write "he thought" or "he felt".

### Three-Camera Method

| Camera | POV | Content | Function |
|------|------|------|------|
| Camera 1—Close-Up | Close-Up | Protagonist's actions, expressions, psychology | Advances main plot, reader immersion |
| Camera 2—Long Shot | Long Shot | Supporting character reactions, environmental changes, onlookers | Expands information surface, creates contrast |
| Camera 3—Voiceover | Voiceover | Setting supplements, background explanation | Provides necessary information in a timely manner |

Camera Alternation Principle: The core alternation is between 1 and 2, with Camera 3 interspersed. Settings should all be triggered by conflict; do not write setting paragraphs in isolation.

### High-Risk Checklist

**POV Red Lines (Strictly Forbidden):**

| Writing Style | Example | Handling |
|------|------|------|
| Omniscient Narrative | "At this time, he still didn't know that three days later..." | Delete, stay in the present |
| God's POV | "If she knew the truth..." | Delete, only write what the protagonist knows |
| Psychological Analysis | "Deep down, he actually..." | Delete, externalize through actions |

**High-Risk Word Replacements:**

| Forbidden | Replacement |
|------|------|
| He felt nervous | His hands were shaking |
| She was very sad | She turned around, shoulders trembling slightly |
| He took a deep breath | He crushed his cigarette in the ashtray |
| A flash of surprise passed through her eyes | Her hand stopped in mid-air |
| As if / like / a trace of / a touch of | Delete or use plain description |
| Flashed through eyes / corners of mouth hooked up / brows slightly furrowed | Replace with specific actions |
| Unquestionable / imperceptible | Delete, directly state the facts |

**High-Risk Sentence Patterns:**

| Pattern | Handling |
|------|------|
| "..., with..." ("He said, with a trace of helplessness") | Delete "with", replace with action |
| "He/She knew..." | Show directly, don't tell |
| Universal metaphors / clichés | Change to everyday metaphors or plain description |
