# Scene Transitions

> A transition is not about omitting the process, but making the reader voluntarily ignore the spatial-temporal shift. Each technique starts with a `[Tag]`.

## Injection Timing

Scene transitions are automatically matched with corresponding tags and loaded when the following conditions trigger:

| Trigger Condition | Judgment Criteria | Example |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Time Jump** | Significant time gap between scenes (hours/days/months) | Fight ends → Next morning, Entering seclusion → Exiting seclusion |
| **Spatial Switch** | Protagonist moves from one location to another | Leaving teahouse → Riverside pavilion, From city to suburbs |
| **Chapter/Section Transition** | Need to bridge past and future at the end of a chapter or section break | Chapter-end suspense, Time span between volumes |
| **Dual-Line/Ensemble Switch** | Alternating narration of multiple story lines, switching POV or scene | Crowd discussing in tavern → Villains plotting in dungeon |
| **Flashback/Retrospection** | Cutting from current timeline to past events | Seeing an old object triggers memory, character recounting the past |

## Tag Selection Guide

| Scene Characteristics | Matching Tags | Selection Reason |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Emotion needs to continue, cannot break | [Emotional Continuation] | Retain residual emotion from the previous scene into the new one |
| Need to hook readers at the end of a chapter | [Crisis Hook] | Throw out suspense; readers will automatically ignore the spatial-temporal jump |
| Sensory gaps like fainting/day-night/teleporting | [Sensory Contrast] | Use 2-3 sensory contrasts instead of straightforward time explanations |
| Cross-time-and-space / flashbacks / long-term foreshadowing | [Object Clue] | Share a landmark object to link two scenes |
| Changing locations in the same city/district | [Dynamic Camera Movement] | Follow the trajectory naturally, cutting out travel details |
| Passage of time during seclusion / healing / lying low | [B-Roll Scenery] | Hint at time through environmental and scenery changes |
| Ensemble cast / dual-line narrative switching | [Dialogue Continuation] | Seamlessly link two lines with the same line of dialogue |


## [Emotional Continuation] Retain Residual Emotion, No Emotional Breaks

**Core:** Retain the residual emotion from the previous scene and carry it into the new scene. It is forbidden for emotions to completely sever or for the character's emotion to reset to zero between consecutive scenes.

### Execution Rules

- The beginning of the new scene first picks up the emotional aftermath of the previous scene, then transitions into the new atmosphere.
- Emotions can be anger, fear, joy, anxiety, or any state.
- Environmental elements (incense in a car, cold wind, tea, etc.) can be used to help calm or continue the emotion.

```
❌ The male protagonist was very angry after a fight; two days later, he went to the auction house to find bargains.

✅ The residual agitation from the fight coiled in his heart until the luxury car stopped in front of the auction house.
   The agarwood incense inside the car smoothed out his hostile aura; he wiped the blood from between his fingers and pushed the door to enter.
```

**Forbidden:** Emotional gaps resetting to zero; straightforward jumping with "three days later, two days later".


## [Crisis Hook] Chapter-End Suspense Hook to Retain Readers

**Core:** Throw out suspense or a crisis at the end of the previous scene; readers focusing on the character's safety will automatically ignore the spatial-temporal jump process.

### Execution Rules

- Set an unresolved crisis at the end of a chapter or at a scene switch point.
- Crisis types: sudden incident, unexpected news, approaching danger, etc.
- The new scene naturally picks up through the "unfolding of consequences" without needing to explain the time transition.

```
❌ The female protagonist received a call during dinner that her grandmother was critically ill, and immediately rushed to the hospital.

✅ The candlelight hadn't yet extinguished when the emergency call popped up; the female protagonist knocked over her water glass in a panic,
   rushing out of the private room regardless of the water stains. The noisy chatter in her ears faded,
   leaving only the cold lights of the hospital and the pungent smell of disinfectant.
```

**Forbidden:** Throwing out a crisis without sufficient buildup, confusing the reader; cutting directly to the next paragraph with no continuation after the suspense is resolved.


## [Sensory Contrast] Sensory Contrast Replacing Time Explanations

**Core:** Do not write about the passage of time straightforwardly; choose 2-3 senses (auditory/tactile/olfactory) for a before-and-after contrast, exercising restraint to avoid piling them up. Suitable for fainting/waking up, day/night transitions, teleportation, etc.

### Execution Rules

- Lock onto major change channels: Auditory (noisy → silent), Tactile (rough → soft), Olfactory (vegetation → mugwort), Light (dark → bright).
- Senses transition from the residue of the previous scene → gradual change → switching to an entirely new scene atmosphere.
- Use a maximum of 3 senses; excessive piling will break immersion.

```
❌ The female protagonist fainted in the forest and woke up after being rescued to a bed and breakfast.

✅ The noisy rustling of the forest slowly dissipated, and the prickly sensation of gravel on her back faded,
   replaced by the warmth of a cotton-linen cushion. The damp smell of vegetation cleared,
   and the scent of mugwort tea drifted into her nose. She opened her eyes to the warm lights of a bed and breakfast.
```

**Forbidden:** Piling up more than 3 senses; straightforwardly transitioning with "when she woke up again"; chaotic rhythm of sensory changes.


## [Object Clue] Shared Objects Linking Across Time and Space

**Core:** Use a signature small object to link two scenes, replacing voiceovers like "years passed, once upon a time", while also serving as foreshadowing.

### Execution Rules

- Object selection must possess: Uniqueness (easy for readers to remember), Tangibility (has texture), and Narrative Quality (can relate to character experiences).
- The object appears in both scenes in the same or similar way (rubbing, staring at, bringing up, etc.).
- Cross-time-and-space scenes: trigger memories or act as a time coordinate through the object.

```
❌ Five years passed; the female protagonist returned to the old street and remembered their encounter as youths.

✅ Her fingertips rubbed the faded peach wood safety charm, the bumpy texture just like back then.
   The wind blew the fallen leaves of the parasol tree, and the scene of the street before her eyes superimposed with the figure of the youth at the entrance of the alley.
```

**Forbidden:** Using cliché objects (jade pendants, lover's knots with generic styles and no recognizability); an object appearing once and never being echoed again.


## [Dynamic Camera Movement] Following the Action Trajectory to Switch Scenes

**Core:** Follow the protagonist's action trajectory to naturally switch scenes; keep only the key action nodes and cut out redundant travel details.

### Execution Rules

- Starting point → 1-2 sensory anchors along the way (light changes, sound fading, temperature differences) → Endpoint.
- Only write "leaving somewhere → key perception along the way → arriving somewhere"; omit "went out the door, turned a corner, and walked for how long".
- Hint at spatial movement through the gradual change of environmental elements.

```
❌ The male protagonist left the teahouse and went to the riverside pavilion to clear his mind.

✅ The male protagonist crushed out his cigarette butt and walked out from under the teahouse eaves, avoiding the crowd to walk slowly along the bluestone riverside.
   The cool breeze of the river brushed his face, and looking up, he was already at the waterfront pavilion.
```

**Forbidden:** Describing irrelevant details along the way in detail (traffic lights, lists of passing shops); straightforwardly writing "he walked for xx minutes and arrived".


## [B-Roll Scenery] Environmental Changes Hinting at Time

**Core:** Rely on environmental and scenery changes to hint at the passage of time. Suitable for seclusion, healing, lying low, or other plots that need to skip time while preserving atmosphere.

### Execution Rules

- Choose significant signs of seasonal/astronomical/plant changes (flowers blooming → falling, summer wind → autumn wind, sunrise → moonrise).
- The greater the span of time passed, the more significant the chosen scenery changes should be.
- Express the sense of time through changes in the same reference point (wind through the door crack, tree outside the window, fire in the stove).

```
❌ The male protagonist went into seclusion for twenty days and came out fully healed.

✅ At the beginning of his seclusion, the hot summer wind poured in through the crack in the door. By the time the locust flowers in the courtyard had all fallen,
   the evening wind carried the chill of autumn. He pushed open the stone door, his old injuries completely healed.
```

**Forbidden:** Directly writing "xx days/months/years later" instead of scenery description; choosing scenery that is irrelevant to the character's state.


## [Dialogue Continuation] Same Dialogue Switching Dual Lines

**Core:** Use the same line of dialogue to connect two plot lines, seamlessly switching between multiple characters/scenes without needing voiceovers like "at the same time".

### Execution Rules

- The dialogue at the end of the previous scene is repeated/echoed in the following scene.
- There must be a logical connection between the two lines (a shared positive goal, or hostile game on the negative side).
- The repeated dialogue can be: exactly the same, partially the same, or semantically corresponding.

```
❌ The crowd in the tavern planned to snatch the treasure, while on the other side, the villain's stronghold also received the news.

✅ "Tomorrow we head to the auction house to snatch the treasure" — the same moment the voice in the tavern fell,
   in the dark dungeon, the villain toyed with a jade pendant and repeated the exact same words.
```

**Forbidden:** Forcibly splicing completely unrelated dialogue; using this between plot lines that have no connection whatsoever.

## Self-Check Mnemonic

```
Transitions don't rely on skipping time, nor on symbols to simply rhyme;
Either emotions carry through, or an object links the two;
Senses shift the atmosphere, dialogue brings both sides near;
B-roll shows the passing year.
```
