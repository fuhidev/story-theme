# Prose Techniques (Universal)

> The core of web novel prose lies not in gorgeousness, but in visual imagery and immersion. These four universal techniques apply to all scene types, and the prompt-crafter always loads this file.


## 1. Abstract to Concrete — Making the Vague Clear

**Core:** "A very expensive instrument, designer clothes, exquisite dishes"—descriptions like these are pixelated in the reader's eyes. Replace abstract words with specific details, and the sense of reality emerges.

```
❌ He took out a very expensive instrument.
✅ An agarwood guqin, entirely pitch-black and gleaming with a cold light.

❌ He wore designer clothes.
✅ A well-tailored dark blue Armani suit, the cufflinks shimmering faintly under the lights.

❌ There were several exquisite dishes on the table.
✅ In the plate lay a deboned King Crab shell drizzled with a golden-yellow glaze.
```

**Key Point:** Search for related images or professional terms before writing and fill them in. You don't need to know everything, but what you write must be specific.


## 2. Camera Switching and Pacing — Writing Like a Director

### Long and Close Shot Switching

Visual writing: First give a long shot (environment), then a medium shot (character), and finally a close-up (detail/expression).

```
Long Shot: The magnificent hall was brightly lit, the music melodious.
Medium Shot: The man in the seat of honor held a wine glass, chatting and laughing spiritedly with the people around him.
Close-Up: His fingers gripping the stem of the glass were pale at the knuckles, trembling slightly.
```

### Combining Long and Short Sentences

Use long sentences to narrate background and build emotion; switch to short sentences for climaxes, reversals, and combat actions.

```
The sky full of sword qi poured down like heavy rain, obscuring the entire line of sight—
That was the ultimate move he had practiced bitterly for thirty years. (Long sentence buildup)
The sword fell. The head rolled.
```


## 3. Killing Adjectives with Verbs

**Core:** Adjectives are dead; verbs are alive. Verbs bring action, and action brings imagery.

```
❌ He was very angry, absolutely furious.

✅ He suddenly slammed the table and stood up; because he used too much force, his knuckles turned slightly blue,
   the teacup on the table was jolted, and tea splashed all over the floor.

❌ An extremely brutal, despicable, and shameless villain.

✅ When the blade fell, he was smiling. His eyes were as flat as if he were handling a daily chore.
```

**Do not write conclusions** (he was very sad); write the imagery (he lit a cigarette with trembling hands, crouched by the roadside, and buried his head in his knees).


## 4. Sensory Superposition Method — Mobilizing the Five Senses

**Core:** Most beginners only write visually (what is seen), but human perception is all-encompassing. Adding one more sense in key scenes doubles the immersion.

| Sense | Usage | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Visual | What is seen | Foundational, indispensable |
| Auditory | What is heard (water dripping / footsteps / breathing) | Creates spatial awareness |
| Tactile | What the skin feels (cold wind / temperature / texture) | Strongest immersion |
| Olfactory | What is smelled (rust / burning / perfume) | Easiest to overlook but most powerful |

```
❌ Thin Visual Version: The cave was very dark, there were stones everywhere, and there was a stone pillar in the middle.

✅ Four-Sense Superposition Version:
   [Visual] The beam of the flashlight pierced the darkness, illuminating the damp moss on the rock wall.
   [Auditory] The sound of dripping water came from deep in the darkness, the echoes long, making the cave seem exceptionally dead and silent.
   [Tactile] A blast of cold wind drilled down the collar, raising a layer of goosebumps.
   [Olfactory] The air was filled with the stale smell of rotting dirt.
```

**Key Point:** You don't need to use all five senses in every paragraph. In key transitions, combat scenes, or emotional outbursts, adding even one extra sense will elevate the texture of the writing by a tier.


## 5. Three-Dimensional Weaving Method — Weaving Occurrence, Perception, and Reaction into the Same Paragraph

**Core:** Every narrative unit (an action / an event / a camera shot) simultaneously contains three dimensions—Occurrence (event progression), Perception (what the protagonist notices), and Reaction (bodily response)—woven into the same paragraph.

One of the most obvious AI fingerprints is splitting these three dimensions into three separate paragraphs. Three-paragraph stacking = instantly recognizable AI flavor.

### Anti-Pattern: Three-Paragraph Stacking (Strictly Forbidden)

```
❌ Three-Paragraph Stacking (AI Flavor):
   Father Lin lowered his head, pressed the document with his left hand, held the pen in his right, and brought it down to the paper.
   His hand was shaking. Not a small shake, but the kind from elbow to wrist.
   The pen tip paused on the paper, wrote a horizontal stroke, and paused again. That left-falling stroke of the character "Lin" was written crookedly.

✅ Woven Style:
   Father Lin pressed the document with his left hand and brought the pen down to the paper with his right; the moment the tip touched the surface, it veered off,
   shaking uncontrollably from elbow to wrist, and that horizontal stroke was dragged out diagonally.
```

| Dimension | Content | Key Points |
|------|------|------|
| Occurrence | The event has occurred | 1-2 sentences, containing a specific detail |
| Perception | What the protagonist notices | ≥1 sensory detail (visual + tactile/auditory/olfactory), focusing on one object or body part |
| Reaction | How the body responds | Specific bodily action (hands/eyes/breathing/posture), can contain one brief psychological freeze-frame |

### Paragraphing Rules

Three-dimensional weaving does not mean stuffing everything into one long paragraph. **Paragraph breaks follow camera/information changes, not dimension changes:**

| Trigger Condition | Paragraphing Method |
|---------|---------|
| New action begins | Start a new paragraph |
| New object/clue appears | Start a new paragraph, carrying that information separately |
| Character's line of sight shifts | Start a new paragraph, forming a camera switch |
| Direct quotation appears | Dialogue gets its own paragraph |
| Psychological freeze-frame / judgment falls | Can be a single-sentence paragraph |

**Guidelines (Not strict rules):**
- One paragraph is recommended to carry one camera beat / one action unit, but reasonable information density is allowed.
- Single paragraph exceeding 80 words → Consider whether it needs splitting based on action or information changes.
- Single sentence exceeding 50 words (unless deliberately slowing the pace) → Recommended to split into two sentences.
- Single-sentence paragraphs are used for pacing emphasis; recommended to not exceed 30% of the entire text.
- **Do not split a woven paragraph into "action paragraph / perception paragraph / reaction paragraph"**.

### Self-Check: Three-Finger Coverage Method

After writing a paragraph, cover the information of the three dimensions separately:

| Cover | Check |
|------|------|
| Cover "Occurrence" | Can you tell the event is progressing in this paragraph? |
| Cover "Perception" | Are there perception details from the character's subjective POV in this paragraph? |
| Cover "Reaction" | Is there a response at the bodily level in this paragraph? |

Whichever dimension is missing, weave the corresponding details back into the same paragraph—**do not start a new paragraph to add it**.
