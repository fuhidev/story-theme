# Character Death / Exit Writing

> Killing off a character is not about making them disappear, but letting them live forever in the reader's heart in another way. Three high-tier methods for writing deaths.

## Injection Timing

| Trigger Condition | Judgment Criteria |
|---------|---------|

## [Regret] Just a Little Bit Short — The Pinnacle of Regret

**Core:** The character is only one second away from embracing the light, but remains in the dark forever. Give the reader immense hope, only to snuff it out instantly at its peak.

### Execution Details

- Lay down a lot of anticipation beforehand: the bowl of noodles they wanted to eat, the person they wanted to see, the false charges they wanted to clear.
- Pull hope to the absolute maximum, then suddenly take everything away.
- What makes the reader sad is not the death itself, but "that text message that will never be sent".

```
❌ The protagonist completed the mission and died with a smile.

✅ He endured humiliation for ten years and finally obtained the evidence sufficient for conviction.
   He took out his phone and sent a text message to the girl who had waited for him for ten years—
   "The mission is over, I will marry you tomorrow."
   One second before pressing send, a bullet pierced his chest.
```

**Key Point:** The regret of falling just a little bit short will stick in the reader's heart like a thorn for years.


## [Delayed Truth] Dying with Misunderstandings — The Delay of Truth

**Core:** The character dies carrying misunderstandings and infamy, perhaps even seeming to deserve it. The truth is only revealed after their death, turning the previous hatred into doubled guilt.

### Execution Details

- In life, the character did many things that made the protagonist misunderstand and resent them—behind every bad deed hid a deeply affectionate truth.
- Uncover the long-buried truth through relics, memory fragments, or the enemy's tone.
- Give the reader three stages of emotion: feeling they deserved to die → confusion → utter breakdown.

```
❌ The villain died, the protagonist breathed a sigh of relief, and everything ended.

✅ He kept hunting Sasuke down, using lethal moves at every turn.
   When he fell, Sasuke even felt relieved.
   It wasn't until later that Sasuke discovered—that older brother who was hunting him down,
   was carrying a lifetime of infamy just to protect him.
   All the ruthlessness was to keep him safe,
   all the infamy was to hold up an umbrella for him in the dark.
```

**Forbidden:** The character dies too quickly, the truth is revealed immediately, leaving no room for guilt to ferment.


## [Flat Aftertaste] The Ultimate Flatness — The Lingering Effect

**Core:** Do not write how earth-shattering the moment of death is. The truly ultimate emotion is silent—after the character dies, the world still turns, but there are empty spaces of their absence everywhere.

### Execution Details

- The moment of death can be written very suddenly and flatly.
- The real knife comes after the funeral—writing about the empty space by "breaking a habit".
- Do not write about sadness; write about subconscious behaviors, empty corners, and things that can never be sent out again.

```
❌ The lover passed away, and the protagonist wept bitterly, overwhelmed with grief.

✅ He returned home and habitually shouted, "Mom, I'm back."
   Then he froze—no one would answer anymore.

   Years later, he achieved success and stood among thousands of people.
   He subconsciously looked towards that corner—no matter success or failure in the past,
   that person was always standing there smiling at him.
   Now that corner was empty, even growing moss.
```

**Key Point:** Things remain but people are gone. Do not write about death directly; write about the empty space caused by death. This slow-burning knife technique can still make people tear up years after reading the book.
