# Dialogue Scene Writing

> Each technique starts with a `[Tag]`. The prompt-crafter matches tags based on the core event of the scene, selecting only the corresponding techniques to inject into the Output·Writing Guidelines.

## Tag Selection Guide

Analyze the **core event** and **character states** of the scene in the Input·Scene Raw Material, and match appropriate tags according to the following rules:

| Scene Characteristics | Matching Tags | Selection Reason |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Large gap in character identities (boss/subordinate, elder/junior, noble/commoner) | [Identity] | People of different identities use different wording; avoid uniform speech patterns. |
| Characters are concealing, lying, evading, or holding back words | [Subtext] | Saying A but actually wanting B; using objects as metaphors for events. |
| Characters are opposing, confronting, negotiating, or fighting for control of the conversation | [Tug-of-war] | Break the Q&A format; use rhetorical questions / emotional cut-offs to create tension. |
| Characters are arguing, pressing for answers, interrogating, or laying cards on the table | [Tug-of-war] [Subtext] | Confrontation + concealment exist simultaneously. |
| More than 4 consecutive lines of pure dialogue | [Action] | Insert actions/expressions to prevent readers from losing the visual sense of the scene. |
| Two characters chatting casually, daily communication, non-conflict scenes | [Action] [Identity] | Use interspersed actions and identity differentiation to maintain the visual scene. |
| Character has inner thoughts, desires, but doesn't voice them | [Subtext] [Tug-of-war] → Irrelevant Answer | Contradiction between inner thoughts and dialogue. |

**Selection Quantity:** Select 2-4 items per scene. If the scene matches multiple tags simultaneously, prioritize the 2 most related to the core event.




## [Identity] Identity-Based Language — Say Goodbye to Uniform Speech Patterns

**Core:** People of different identities say different things. If the character's name is covered, the reader should still be able to recognize who is speaking from their manner of speech.

### Execution Details

| Identity | Speech Characteristics | Pitfall Avoidance |
|------|---------|------|
| **Ordinary Commoner** | Slang, short sentences, many interjections, loose word order, few written vocabulary words | Do not use neat, long sentences |
| **Intellectual / Superior** | Euphemistic wording, leaves room to maneuver, habitually expresses indirectly, avoids absolute statements | Do not speak too plainly and directly |
| **Hardboiled Character (Assassin / Enemy)** | Extremely minimalist wording, flat tone, few emotional words, outwardly indifferent | Do not vent emotions |

### Template Sentences + Practical Examples

```
❌ You're fired, don't come in tomorrow.

✅ He lit a cigarette, not in a hurry to speak. Only when the smoke dispersed did he open his mouth, his tone flat. "You know the company's difficulties right now; there's no room for you here anymore. Go find another way out."
```

**Pitfall Avoidance:** Different identity characters sharing the same speaking style; writing low-class character dialogue using formal written language.


## [Subtext] Subtext & Contrasting Tension — The Core of Game/Confrontation

**Core Logic:** Saying A with the mouth, but actually wanting B. Absolutely do not directly state the core purpose, such as requests, hatred, breaking up, or deals.

### Technique 1: Using Objects as Metaphors

Replace the core conflict with scenery/food/objects currently in sight.

```
❌ Let's get a divorce; I don't want to live like this anymore.

✅ He picked up a piece of fatty meat for her. "This meat has been stewing all afternoon, it's completely mushy, it should be thrown away."
He stared at the plate without moving. "It's not cold yet, it's barely edible, and our days are the same."
```

### Technique 2: Insincere Words

Extreme inner opposition, but the tone is surprisingly calm and gentle.

**Pitfall Avoidance:** Characters straightforwardly revealing their true feelings, arguing directly; conflict points entirely pointed out via dialogue.


## [Tug-of-war] Breaking the Q&A Format — Creating a Sense of Tug-of-war

**Core:** Characters do not answer according to the other party's expectations, creating unpredictability.

### Technique 1: Irrelevant Answer

> Applicable: Evading questions, unwilling to state a position, inner conflict

**Execution:** Do not respond to the question; jump to irrelevant scenery/events, using silence + digressions to evade.

```
"Do you actually love me or not?"
He walked to the window, looking at the rain outside. "If this rain doesn't stop, the crops down south will be completely ruined."
```

### Technique 2: Rhetorical Suppression

> Applicable: Confrontation, probing, seizing conversational control, aggressive game

**Execution:** Do not admit/deny directly; throw the question back at the other party, adding an implied interrogation.

```
"Did you do this?"
She didn't look up, continuing to flip through the documents in her hand. "What do you think? Besides you, who else in this room could be so ruthless?"
```

### Technique 3: Emotional Cut-off

> Applicable: Enemies meeting, life-or-death confrontation, contempt from a higher-up

**Execution:** The other party releases intense emotions, while this side ignores the conflict, focuses on an irrelevant trifle, and then responds nonchalantly.

```
"Ten years of hatred, today I will definitely kill you!"
He lowered his head and blew on the soup in his bowl, frowning. "The soup is too salty, and the noodles are overcooked. What did you just say? Things from ten years ago, I've long forgotten. Sit down and try the chili oil here?"
```

**Pitfall Avoidance:** Mechanical Q&A style dialogue; both sides simultaneously amplifying emotions and yelling at each other in conflict scenes.


## [Action] Dialogue + Action/Environment Interspersion — Escaping Pure Script-Style Lines

**Core:** Between every 1-2 lines of dialogue, insert 1 micro-action/expression/environmental detail.

### Detail Selection Principles

The action and the emotion of the dialogue should form a contrast: nervous → casual, lying → subconscious small actions.

### Universal Action Library (Direct Call)

| Emotion | Action |
|------|-------|
| Guilty / Lying | Grabbing thighs, trimming nails, flicking ash onto oneself, shifting gaze |
| Indifferent / Contemptuous | Keeping head down to work, not looking up, fiddling with objects at hand |
| Suppressed / Enduring | Clenching fingers, rolling Adam's apple, pausing in silence |

### Example Sentence Template

```
His fingertips unconsciously rubbed the rim of the cup. "I can't promise you this."
```

**Writing Key Points:** Action first → Dialogue second → Action hints at emotion, not explained by dialogue. No brackets needed for annotations.

**Pitfall Avoidance:** A whole paragraph of continuous quoted dialogue without any supplementary actions or expressions.
