# World Setting Guide

> Discuss three aspects (Geography / Politics / Rules), and immediately write them into `settings/world-setting.md` after discussing.
> Do not explain concepts, just chat directly. After discussing each aspect, go through the self-check in this section before moving to the next.

---

## Principle 1: Completely Fictional

**Rule:** If the author mentions a real place name, dynasty, or historical figure, stop immediately and replace it.

✅ Correct:
> Author: "The background is similar to the Tang Dynasty."
> → "Tang Dynasty cannot be used directly. What is your world called? What is the dynasty called?"

❌ Incorrect:
> Author: "The background is similar to the Tang Dynasty."
> → "Okay, we will set it according to the Tang Dynasty." (Directly used the real setting)

**Judgment Criteria:** After writing, do a full-text search for real vocabulary like "China / Beijing / Tang Dynasty / Ming Dynasty / Paris / New York". If there is even one → send back for rewrite, there cannot be a single one.

---

## Principle 2: Start from Scenarios, Don't Draw Maps

**Rule:** Do not first discuss "how big the world is", first ask "where does the protagonist appear in the first chapter". Expand outwards from there.

✅ Correct:
> Q: "Where does the protagonist appear in the first chapter?"
> A: "Qingzhou City." → "What is Qingzhou City like? Surrounded by mountains on three sides or a flat plain?"
> After discussing the city → "What is outside the city? How far is the nearest place? How to get there?"

❌ Incorrect:
> "Let's draw a world map first — how big is the continent, how many countries, where are the borders."
> (The author filled in a bunch of geographical nouns, but doesn't know where to use them in the first chapter)

**Judgment Criteria:** Every location in the geography section can find a corresponding scene or route in the first chapter. If there is one that cannot be used in the first chapter → delete it first, add it when used.

## Discussing the Three Aspects Item by Item

### Discussing Geography: Where is the Protagonist → How to Go → What is the Road Like

If geography is discussed clearly, the agent will know where the character stands and what is around them when writing the scene. Asking these three questions in order is enough:

1. "Where does the protagonist mainly operate? City/Village/Sect/Planet? What is it like?"
2. "Where do they need to go? How far? How to go? Walk/Ride horse/Teleportation array?"
3. "Is there anything special on the way? (Mountains/Rivers/Checkpoints/Demonic beasts/Inspection stations)"

After chatting, check if the levels are sufficient according to the genre:

| Genre | Must Discuss At Least |
|------|---------|
| Sci-Fi/Interstellar | Planet + City + Star Sector Background |
| Xianxia/Fantasy | City/Market + Sect/Cave Dwelling |
| Western Fantasy | Town + Village + Wilderness |
| Ancient Politics | City + Prefecture/County + Official Road |
| Urban/Era | City/District + Street + Neighborhood |
| Post-Apocalyptic/Infinite | Stronghold + Exploration Area + Safe Zone Boundary |

✅ Correct → You can imagine the space with your eyes closed:
> Qingzhou City is located at the eastern foot of the Cangwu Mountain Range, surrounded by mountains on three sides and water on one side. Fifteen miles southeast of the city is the Qingjiang Ferry, which is the lifeline of merchants. Crossing the Cangwu Mountain to the west is a wasteland rampant with demonic beasts.

❌ Incorrect → Data list, no sense of picture:
> Qingzhou City is located on the east side of the Cangwu Mountain Range, covering an area of about 200 square kilometers with a population of about 100,000. It is a major town in Jiangnan Circuit.

**Judgment Criteria:** Close your eyes and you can say "what you pass from A to B, how far, what it feels like". Cannot say it → not specific enough.

**Content to write into the file (Reference Checklist):**
- Name of main scenes, location relationships (how far between city/village/secret realm, how to go)
- Climate and seasonal characteristics (four seasons change, extreme weather)
- Geographical restrictions (mountains/waters/borders — what blocks different areas)
- Ways and difficulty of passing between areas

**Section Self-Check (Pass before entering Politics):**
- [ ] Does every location correspond to a scene or route in the first chapter?
- [ ] If you remove the geography paragraph, is the picture still there? (Yes → written correctly; No → this is an instruction manual)
- [ ] Did you at least ask "where → how to go → what is the road like"?

---

### Discussing Politics: Who Has the Final Say → Who Limits Them → What Happens If You Disobey

Politics determines how free the character can be, and who they will be in trouble with if provoked. Direction proposals and chapter outlines rely on this to judge conflict and cooperation. Ask these three questions in order:

1. "Who has the final say in your story? Emperor / Sect Master / Council of Elders / Company CEO? Is their power absolute or restricted?"
2. "What power can restrict them? Another faction / Law / Public opinion / Heavenly Dao / Money?"
3. "What happens to ordinary people if they disobey? Arrested / Expelled / Beaten / Actually nothing happens?"

If the author doesn't know where to start, give a starting point based on the genre:

| Genre | Where to Start Discussing |
|------|------------|
| Xianxia Sect | "Sect or Dynasty? Two parallel systems or just one?" |
| Ancient Politics | "Are there factions within the royal court? Who is fighting with whom over what?" |
| Urban Gangs | "Underground forces or official agencies? How many forces?" |
| Interstellar/Empire | "Centralized power or Federation? What is the relationship between local and central?" |
| Harem/Mansion | "How clearly is legitimate and illegitimate divided? What is the upward mobility channel?" |

✅ Correct → Has forces, stances, and checks & balances:
> Three forces: The government maintains order but has limited power; the Qing Gang controls the docks and wrestles with the government in the dark; the outer sect of Xuantian Sect is aloof from worldly affairs. Any alliance of two parties will break the balance.

❌ Incorrect → Only writes the official legal structure, doesn't write who actually has the final say:
> The court set up a prefecture system, Qingzhou Prefecture belongs to Jiangnan Circuit. Prefect Chen Boyuan holds military and political power.

**Judgment Criteria:** Can clearly answer the three "whos" — who manages the protagonist, who protects the protagonist, who the protagonist will be in trouble with if provoked. Cannot answer → not enough forces discussed.

**Content to write into the file (Reference Checklist):**
- Form of rule (Centralized / Enfeoffment / Sect / City-state? Who has the final say? Is it restricted?)
- List of main forces (At least 2-3, describe the stance and demands of each in one sentence)
- Balance of power between forces (Who is stronger and who is weaker? Cooperative or antagonistic?)
- Social stratification (Commoner / Noble / Cultivator / Slave? How to move between classes?)
- Cost of disobedience (What happens if rules are violated? Who executes the punishment?)

**Section Self-Check (Pass before entering Rules):**
- [ ] Can name at least 2 forces, each explaining "what they want" in one sentence?
- [ ] Are there specific descriptions of the consequences for ordinary people breaking the rules?
- [ ] Do the rulers have any limitations or checks and balances?

### Discussing Rules: Ask What Cannot Be Done First, Then Ask What Can Be Done

Rules restrict the character, then the conflict is established. Direction proposals rely on rules to judge what the character can and cannot do, and chapter outlines rely on rules to determine the resolution of conflicts. **Restrictions are more important than abilities.** First ask "what cannot be done", then ask "can special things be done".

Rules are divided into three levels, each with a different scope of impact:

| Level | Scope | Example | Exceptions |
|------|------|------|------|
| **World Level** | Effective for everyone | Heavenly Dao laws, physical rules, magic origin | Almost none, if there is, it's a worldview loophole |
| **Social Level** | Effective for specific groups | Laws, sect rules, industry rules, taboos | Yes — people with great power can bypass |
| **Personal Level** | Only effective for specific characters | Bloodline curse, cultivation art limits, contract oaths | Yes — finding a way to break it is the plot |

Ask in this order:

1. **World Level: "What underlying restrictions does this world have? What can everyone NOT do?"** (No magic / cannot resurrect / Heavenly Dao is flawed)
2. **Social Level: "What laws or rules must characters obey? What happens if they violate them?"** (Deduce from the results of the politics discussion)
3. **Personal Level: "Does the protagonist have any special restrictions — bloodline curse / cultivation art flaws / contract oath?"**

Each rule must have three elements, indispensable:

| Element | How to Ask | Consequence of Not Writing |
|------|------|-----------|
| What can be done | "What ability or permission does this rule give them?" | Readers don't know what use the rule has |
| What cannot be done | "Under what circumstances is it invalid? What are the limiting conditions?" | The protagonist is omnipotent |
| Cost | "What will be paid after using it?" | Readers think "Then just use it, why not?" — no tension |

✅ Correct → Has restrictions, has costs:
> Those above the Golden Core stage are bound by the Heavenly Dao oath and cannot attack mortals — violators will face Heavenly Tribulation. Each major realm promotion consumes lifespan — Foundation Establishment subtracts ten years, Nascent Soul subtracts fifty years.

❌ Incorrect → Only ability description, no restrictions or costs:
> Cultivation system: Qi Refining → Foundation Establishment → Golden Core → Nascent Soul. Promotion at each level requires heavenly materials and earthly treasures + cultivation arts + fortuitous encounters.

**Judgment Criteria:** When reading the rules, the reader will think "Then it's better not to use it lightly" (has cost) or "I really want to know how they break through" (has restrictions). If after reading they feel "Then just use it, why not?" → The cost is not written enough.

**Content to write into the file (Reference Checklist):**
- World Level Rules — Core description of the power system / Heavenly Dao / magic / technology
- Social Level Rules — Laws / Taboos / Industry rules (at least 2 specific rules)
- Personal Level Rules — Special restrictions affecting the character (if any)
- Three elements of each rule: What can be done, what cannot be done, cost
- Exceptions to rules: Are there exceptions? Who can be the exception? Why?

**Section Self-Check (Pass before entering output):**
- [ ] Both world-level rules and social-level rules exist? (At least two levels of coverage)
- [ ] Each rule has three elements: what can be done, what cannot be done, cost?
- [ ] Are the exceptions to the rules explained? (Who can be the exception? Why?)

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Manifestation | How to Fix |
|------|------|------|
| Instruction manual listing | "Population 100,000, area 200 square kilometers" | Delete data, change to scene description |
| Only writing ability, no cost | "Golden Core stage flies on sword" → Then why not fly all the time? | Add cost: consumes spiritual power / risk of falling |
| Map-style stacking of place names | Writes ten place names in one breath, nine are useless | Delete until only what is used in the first chapter remains |
| Politics only has official story | "Prefect holds military and political power" → But in reality? | Add real power relationships (who really has the final say) |
| Rules have no exceptions | "No one can fly" → Too absolute, impossible to write later | Add exception: unless with a Wind Control Token / Sect permission |
| Fictional mixed with real | "Similar to the Ming Dynasty's Jinyiwei" | Change name: Xiuyi Bureau / Xuanyi Guard / Supervisory Department |

## Genre Level References

The following is for reference when discussing the corresponding genre, no need to read all at once.

### Geography Levels (Choose based on genre)

```
General scale (from largest to smallest):
Star Sector → Galaxy → Planet → Continent → Dynasty/Alliance → State/County/Territory → City → Block/Ward → Stronghold/Room
```

| Genre | Where the top level starts | Downward expansion direction | Typical level chain |
|------|------------|------------|-----------|
| **Sci-Fi/Interstellar** | Star Sector/Galaxy | → Planet/City | Star Sector → Galaxy → Planet → Alliance → City → District |
| **Xianxia/Fantasy** | Continent/Plane | Two systems: Dynasty manages mortals, Sect manages cultivators | Dynasty → County → City → Village → Individual<br>Sect → Peak → Hall → Individual |
| **Western Fantasy** | Continent/Kingdom | → Territory/Town | Continent → Kingdom → County → Town → Village → Dungeon |
| **Ancient Politics** | World/Country | → Province/Prefecture/City | Central Plains → Country → Province → Prefecture/County → County → City |
| **Urban/Era** | Country/Province | → City/Street | Country → Province → City → District → Street → Neighborhood |
| **Post-Apocalyptic/Infinite** | Stronghold/Area | → Floor/Map | Safe Zone → Sub-area → Stronghold → Exploration Area → Instance |

After determining the top level, expanding downwards **2-3 levels** is enough. Xianxia starts from "Continent" and expanding to "City/Market" is enough; Urban starts from "City" and expanding to "Street" is enough.

### Politics Levels (Choose based on genre)

| Genre | Source of top-level pressure | Downward extension direction | Typical level chain |
|------|------------|------------|-------------|
| **Interstellar/Empire Politics** | Interstellar Alliance/Imperial Parliament | → Planet/Local | Interstellar Parliament → Imperial Royal Family → Planetary Governor → Local Consul → Family |
| **Xianxia Sect** | Cultivation World Structure/Heavenly Court | → Internal Sect/Rogue Cultivators | Cultivation World Covenant/Heavenly Court → Sect → Peak/Hall → Elder → Disciple |
| **Ancient Politics** | Royal Court/Emperor | → Provincial Prefecture/Family | Emperor → Cabinet/Three Departments → Provincial Prefecture Yamen → State/County → Family → Individual |
| **Urban Gangs** | Underground Power Structure | → Branch/Minions | Underworld Alliance → Gang → Branch/Dock → Boss → Minions |
| **Harem/Mansion** | Royal Family/Clan | → Legitimate vs Illegitimate/Servants | Royal Family → Family/Clan → Direct Line/Branch → Main Wife/Legitimate Child → Illegitimate/Servants |

After determining the level, extend upwards one level (larger background pressure) and downwards one level (organization specifically affecting the character).

## Cases

The following three cases cover different genres and can be provided to the author for reference when discussing the corresponding genre.

### Case 1: Xianxia Fantasy

```markdown
## Geography

Qingzhou City is located at the eastern foot of the Cangwu Mountain Range, surrounded by mountains on three sides and water on one side. The city is divided into inner and outer parts: the inner city is the residential area for government offices and wealthy families, the city wall is three zhang high, with four gates, closed at night and opened in the morning; the outer city is the civilian marketplace, without a city wall, built along the river, with streets and alleys crisscrossing like a spider web.

Fifteen miles southeast of the city is the Qingjiang Ferry, which is the lifeline of merchants. Traveling down the river leads to various states in Jiangnan, and traveling up leads deep into the Cangwu Mountains. The ferry opens at the Mao hour (5-7 AM) and closes at the You hour (5-7 PM) every day, and boats do not travel at night.

The Cangwu Mountain Range runs north-south and is a natural barrier between Qingzhou and the Western Border. There are three passable ancient roads in the mountains, but heavy snow seals the mountains in winter, and only small paths familiar to hunters can be taken. Crossing the Cangwu Mountains to the west is a wasteland rampant with demonic beasts — no one has ever crossed it alive.

Climate: Wet, cold, and foggy in winter, the surface of Qingjiang River is filled with morning fog, which dissipates around noon. Hot, humid, and rainy in summer, June to August is the rainy season. When the water level of Qingjiang River surges, the ferry suspends operations for three to five days.

## Politics

Form of rule: The court set up a prefecture system, and Qingzhou Prefecture belongs to Jiangnan Circuit. Prefect Chen Boyuan holds military and political power and is subject to the assessment of the court's Ministry of Personnel.

Three forces:
- **Government** — Maintains order, but power is limited. Five hundred prefecture soldiers, well-equipped but with low morale. The Prefect is restricted by the court's laws and cannot mobilize resources arbitrarily.
- **Qing Gang** — Controls the docks and marketplace businesses, ostensibly cooperating with the government but wrestling in the dark. Gang leader Hong Sihai, with three thousand gang members, has infiltrated the low-level clerks of the prefecture yamen. The Qing Gang dares not openly oppose the government, but can influence the situation through "information gaps".
- **Outer Sect of Xuantian Sect** — A cultivator faction, non-interfering in worldly affairs but possessing a transcendent status. One stationed elder, ten Foundation Establishment disciples. The court has no jurisdiction over cultivators — Xuantian Sect disciples who break the law are handled by the sect itself.

A tripartite balance, any alliance of two parties will break the equilibrium. The government and the Qing Gang check and balance each other, and Xuantian Sect is a variable neither dares to provoke.

Social stratification:
- Cultivators (Transcendent status, not bound by secular laws)
- Officials / Gentry (Have privileges, but bound by court laws)
- Commoners (Pay taxes and perform corvee, no privileges. Offending the Qing Gang might result in a beating, offending the government might result in jail)
- Low Class (Prostitutes, actors, yamen runners, descendants cannot take imperial exams, cannot intermarry with commoners)

Cost of disobedience: Commoners resisting taxes → beaten with boards + jail. Gang members betraying → three knives and six holes (severe gang punishment). Corrupt officials investigated → property confiscated and exiled. Disrespecting Xuantian Sect → no one can help you, the government can't manage it either.

## Rules

### World Level: Cultivation System

Cultivation path: Qi Refining → Foundation Establishment → Golden Core → Nascent Soul. Promotion at each level requires heavenly materials and earthly treasures + cultivation arts + fortuitous encounters, none can be missing.

What can be done: Qi Refining stage strengthens the body, immune to all diseases. Foundation Establishment stage can fly on objects, low-level spells. Golden Core stage can fly on swords, divine sense scans a hundred miles. Nascent Soul stage can let the soul leave the body, travel a hundred miles in an instant.

What cannot be done: Cannot cultivate independently without cultivation arts. Forcing a breakthrough → qi deviation, at best losing all cultivation, at worst sudden death. Those above Golden Core are bound by the Heavenly Dao oath and cannot attack mortals — violators will face Heavenly Tribulation.

Cost: Each major realm promotion consumes lifespan — Foundation Establishment subtracts ten years, Golden Core subtracts thirty years, Nascent Soul subtracts fifty years. When lifespan is exhausted, body and soul are destroyed, cannot enter reincarnation.

### Social Level: Qingzhou City Unspoken Rules

- Mortals must not interfere in cultivator disputes. Reporting to the authorities is useless — the Prefect can't manage it.
- Outsiders are not allowed to do business in Qing Gang's territory. Not paying protection money → shop gets smashed, person gets thrown out of the city.
- Xuantian Sect disciples are not allowed to cast spells within the city. Violators are recalled and disciplined by the sect itself.

### Personal Level: Restrictions on Protagonist Shen Mu

Shen Mu was scratched by a demonic beast in childhood, and demonic qi remains in his body. After the late Foundation Establishment stage, the demonic qi begins to backlash — on the night of the full moon, spiritual power goes out of control, and when emotionally agitated, his pupils turn into vertical slits. During the Golden Core tribulation, the conflict between demonic qi and spiritual qi will mean certain death.
```

### Case 2: Urban Supernatural

```markdown
## Geography

Jiangcheng City, an old industrial city in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River, with a population of eight million. The Yangtze River runs through the city, dividing it into Jiangnan and Jiangbei — Jiangbei is the old town, dense with factories, shantytowns, and old residential buildings; Jiangnan is the new district, with commercial centers, high-end residences, and university towns.

Metro Line 4 runs north-south, connecting Jiangcheng City with the provincial capital, a forty-minute drive. There is a high-speed rail station in the west of the city, and the old railway station in the east — green-skinned trains are still running, connecting several surrounding counties.

Inside the Third Ring Road is the core area of the city, between the Third Ring Road and the beltway is the urban-rural fringe, and outside the beltway are farmlands and townships. Fifty kilometers west of Jiangcheng urban area is the tail of the Dabie Mountains, and there is an abandoned military base in the mountains — now the secret gathering place for super-powered individuals in the city.

Climate: Four distinct seasons, summer high temperatures can reach forty degrees, winter is wet and cold and it occasionally snows. The plum rain season is in June, with continuous rainy weather for half a month.

## Politics

Form of rule: The Jiangcheng municipal government manages daily affairs, but super-powered affairs are secretly managed by the "Special Affairs Management Bureau" (Special Bureau). The Special Bureau reports directly to the central government, is not under the local government, and the Bureau Chief is on the same level as the Municipal Party Secretary.

Three forces:
- **Special Bureau** — The official super-power management agency, registered super-powered individuals are under its jurisdiction and protection. Bureau Chief Zhao Yuanshan is an A-class super-powered individual himself, with five action teams under his command. The goal is to maintain stability and prevent super-powered individuals from being exposed to the public.
- **Dark Web** — The underground trading network for unregistered super-powered individuals. Provides black market super-powered items, intelligence, and bounties. The leader's identity is unknown, only known by the codename "Professor". It is in a delicate balance with the Special Bureau of "you don't touch me, I don't touch you".
- **Guiyuan Society** — A folk organization transformed from an ancient cultivation sect, which believes that super-powers are a mutated branch of ancient martial arts cultivation. There are not many members, but the average strength is high, and they look down on the Special Bureau and the Dark Web. The headquarters is in the abandoned military base in the west of the city.

The three forces distrust each other: the Special Bureau wants to incorporate the Guiyuan Society and crack down on the Dark Web; the Dark Web wants to infiltrate the Special Bureau; the Guiyuan Society feels that both sides are "juniors who do not understand the Dao".

Cost of disobedience: Registered super-powered individuals breaking rules → Special Bureau seals powers + residential surveillance. Dark Web members caught → Special Bureau secret trial, locked into an underground prison. Guiyuan Society disciples acting without authorization → cultivation abolished, expelled from the sect.

## Rules

### World Level: Super-power System

Super-power classification: D → C → B → A → S. The judgment criterion for each level is super-power output power and precision, not cultivated — the level of a super-powered individual is fixed after awakening, they can only improve control through training, cannot raise the upper limit.

What can be done: D-class affects small-range physical phenomena (lighting a cigarette, pushing a cup). C-class affects a single human body (reading surface thoughts, short-distance telekinesis). B-class affects the environment within a building (controlling fire, condensing water into ice). A-class affects a block (short-term weather manipulation, force field shields). S-class affects a city-level range — only seven people known globally.

What cannot be done: Super-powers cannot be inherited. The probability of descendants of super-powered individuals awakening is only five percentage points higher than that of ordinary people. Cannot raise the level upper limit through cultivation / drugs. Forbidden arts (extracting others' super-powers, forcibly merging super-power cores) will result in sudden death, the success rate is less than one in a thousand.

Cost: Using super-powers every time consumes mental power. Overuse → mental sea depletion, at best a migraine for three days, at worst permanent downgrade. After an S-class unleashes a finishing move, they might need to rest for half a year.

### Social Level: Iron Laws of the Super-power World

- Do not expose super-powers in front of ordinary people. Violation → Special Bureau intervenes, warning or sealing depending on the circumstances.
- Registered super-powered individuals report their status to the Special Bureau every quarter. Missing check-in twice in a row → listed as a dangerous missing person.
- Black market super-power trading can be caught or ignored — as long as no lives are lost, the Special Bureau turns a blind eye.

### Personal Level: Restrictions on Protagonist Chen Mo

Chen Mo, a C-class mind reader. He was stimulated during awakening, resulting in unstable abilities — when emotions fluctuate, the mind-reading range goes out of control, and thought fragments of everyone within ten meters flood into his brain, at best a non-stop nosebleed, at worst fainting. After each loss of control, he needs three days to recover.
```

### Case 3: Alternate History Era

```markdown
## Geography

Linhai City, a medium-sized port city on the southeast coast. The old town is built along the river, with old streets of arcade houses crisscrossing. The road surface is bluestone, two lanes wide, and the shops on both sides are next to each other — rice shops, cloth shops, herbal medicine shops, pawn shops.

The east of the city is the dock area, where sea ships and inland river boats meet. The docks are divided into three berthing areas: military, commercial, and fishing. The commercial dock is the largest, handling thousands of tons of cargo daily. Behind the docks are warehouse areas and workers' shantytowns — houses built with wooden slats, five or six family members squeezed into one room.

The west of the city is the new district, with western-style buildings, churches, new-style schools, and post offices — three or four-story small buildings in the style of the Republic of China, with French plane trees planted along the street. Further west are suburban farmlands, growing rice and vegetables to supply the whole city.

The main arterial road running through the city is called Zhongshan Road, going straight from the docks to the west of the city. The widest part of the road can fit two cars side by side — there are only a dozen or so cars in the whole city, most people rely on walking and bicycles.

Climate: Subtropical monsoon climate, high temperatures and heavy rain in summer, frequent typhoons. When a typhoon comes, the docks stop working, and water accumulates over the knees in low-lying areas.

## Politics

Form of rule: Linhai City is under the jurisdiction of Jiangnan Province, and the mayor is appointed by the provincial government. But Linhai is far from the central power, and orders from the province are often discounted when they reach Linhai. The ones who really have the final say are the local Chamber of Commerce and the Dockers' Union.

Three forces:
- **Municipal Government** — Maintains basic operations, collects taxes, maintains law and order, manages education and health. The police station has two hundred people, with outdated equipment. They can manage petty theft, but if something big happens, they can't suppress it. Mayor Zhou Hongsheng is a centrist, listens to whoever gives more money.
- **Linhai Chamber of Commerce** — Controls the docks, money shops, and several largest factories. President Lin Zhongfu, a scholar from the former Qing Dynasty, went into business after the Republic of China, and is extremely wealthy. The Chamber of Commerce maintains a fifty-person security team, equipped better than the police — with phased-out rifles bought from the garrison troops.
- **Dockers' Union** — An organization of dock loaders, with three thousand members. Chairman Zhao Dazhu, a dock worker himself, illiterate but with extremely high prestige. The union can call on dock workers to strike — if the boats don't move, Linhai's economy stops.

Delicate balance: The Chamber of Commerce needs the docks to ship goods, the union needs the Chamber of Commerce to pay wages, and the municipal government needs both to pay taxes. An alliance of any two parties can force the third party to submit — so no one dares to really form an alliance.

Cost of disobedience: Offend the Chamber of Commerce → your shop "accidentally" catches fire. Offend the union → nobody moves your goods. Offend the government → get arrested and locked up for a few days — but that's it, the municipal government lacks the ability to lock people up long-term.

## Rules

### World Level: Rules of the Era

The rules of this world are the **rules of the Republic of China** — there are laws but execution depends on personal connections, money makes the mare go, and political power grows out of the barrel of a gun.

What can be done: With money, you can buy most things — official positions, women, lives. With connections, you can bypass the law. With guns, you can call the shots yourself.

What cannot be done: You cannot openly challenge the interests of foreigners — behind the foreigners are warships. You cannot push things to the absolute extreme — if pushed into a corner, the other party will fight for their life. You cannot openly oppose the government — the label they will put on you is "rebel party".

Cost: Opposing foreigners → the consulate exerts pressure, and the municipal government hands you over. Pushing things to the extreme → the opposing family will fight a "lawsuit" (actually fighting and killing) with you. Being labeled a "rebel party" → the province directly sends troops.

### Social Level: Linhai City Rules

- The union has the final say on matters on the docks, people from the Chamber of Commerce cannot go to the docks and point fingers.
- The Chamber of Commerce's security team cannot enter the dock area — entering is crossing the line, and the union brothers will not agree.
- The police do not touch prominent people — arresting them won't keep them locked up, and it offends people.
- It is a universal truth to pay back debts, but you can find a middleman to plead for mercy and pay in installments.

### Personal Level: Restrictions on Protagonist Ah Sheng

Ah Sheng is a dock worker. His parents died in an "accident" ten years ago — he suspects it was done by people from the Chamber of Commerce, but has no proof. He works at the docks and lives in the territory of the Chamber of Commerce. He sees his enemies every day but cannot fall out with them. He wants revenge, but his wife and children rely on his wages to eat — if they fall out, the whole family starves.
```

## Three-Step Final Check (Run after all three items are discussed)

After completely discussing and writing into the file, go through three steps of self-checking. If it doesn't pass, do not enter the next stage.

### Step 1: Scan the Format

| Check Items | How to Check | If Not What to Do |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Unification of secondary titles | Confirmed to be `## Geography` / `## Politics` / `## Rules`? | Unify to `##` |
| Consistent indentation | Use `###` for third level and `- ` for lists. No mixing? | Unified format |
| No text mixed in | Confirm that the novel text fragments are not mixed into the settings? | Yes → Delete |
| Word count | The full text should not exceed 2,000 words? | More than → Streamline to within 2000 |
| File in correct location | `settings/world-setting.md` exists? | Move to the correct path |

### Step 2: Go Through the Content Item by Item

| Check Items | How to Check | Failure → Downstream Impact |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Completely fictional | Full text search for real place names/dynasties/historical figures, yes → not fictional | Has any → return and rewrite |
