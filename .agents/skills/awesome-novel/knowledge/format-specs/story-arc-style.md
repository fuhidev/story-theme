# Main Story Arc Breakdown Guide

> Phase 2 Step 1 — Determine the skeleton of the entire book, then expand volume by volume. After the breakdown, write it into the `story_arc` section + `volumes` list of `story.md`.
> Total 3 steps: Overall main storyline → Work backwards from the ending to find breakpoints → Determine the conflict for each volume. Go through the full process when entering Phase 2 for the first time, skip it later.

---

## First, Determine the Author Type

Before breaking down the outline, first determine what type of author they are. Ask one question:

> "Do you currently have a complete concept of the story's direction for the entire book — have you thought it through from beginning to end? Or do you only have ideas for the first volume and will see how it goes later?"

Based on the answer, take two paths:

| The Author Said | → Type | Path |
|--------|--------|------|
| "I have thought it all out" "Have a complete concept" "Know from beginning to end" | **A: Knows the whole book direction** | Follow existing process: Overall main storyline → Determine end point → Find breakpoints → Determine each volume's conflict |
| "Only know the beginning" "Only the first volume" "Haven't thought about the later parts" | **B: Only knows the beginning** | Overall main storyline narrowed to "first volume direction" → End point/breakpoints marked "TBD" → Only determine the first volume's conflict |
| "Somewhere in between — have a general direction but no details" | Follow A, but later volume conflicts are allowed to be "TBD" | The end point direction is there, breakpoints might be blurry, later volume conflicts marked "TBD" |

> **Intermediary Type Handling:** The author says there is a direction but no details — follow the Type A process, but allow the later volume's `core_conflict` to be marked "TBD" (same as Type B). The difference is: Type A requires the overall main storyline to be at the whole book level, the end point has a picture (even if blurry); Type B's overall main storyline is at the volume level, the end point is marked "TBD".

---

## Overall Main Storyline

**What to write:**
- **Type A:** The core conflict of the whole book — explain the core conflict of the whole book in one sentence. The standard is **it cannot be answered if all volumes are not completed**.
- **Type B:** The core direction of the first volume — this sentence only covers the first volume, but must contain "who + wants to do what + is hindered by what". It's not the main storyline of the whole book, it's the starting direction.

### Judgment Criteria

**Type A (Whole Book Level):**
- ✅ Contains "who + pursuit + confrontation/overcoming"
- ✅ Cannot be answered after completing just one volume, requires multiple volumes to finish telling
- ❌ "The protagonist gets stronger" — no confrontation, can be finished in one volume
- ❌ "Lu Zheng investigates various cases" — "various cases" is not a single main storyline

**Type B (Volume Level):**
- ✅ Contains "who + wants to do what + is hindered by what"
- ✅ Is a conflict that "can be advanced or resolved within the scope of the first volume"
- ✅ The author can say "what the first volume is roughly about, what the protagonist wants to do"
- ❌ "The protagonist gets stronger" — no confrontation, no specific goal
- ❌ "The protagonist experiences some adventures" — no specific event

### Agent Processing Steps

Do not ask "What is the core conflict of your whole book?" — the author won't be able to answer. **Listen to the story first, then extract the structure.**

**Step 1: Let the author speak**

Choose the phrasing based on the type:

> **Type A:** "The settings are done, now let's plan the structure of the entire story. In your own words, what is this story about from beginning to end? What does the protagonist want to achieve the most?"
>
> **Type B:** "Okay, you already have an idea for the first volume. In your own words, what is the first volume roughly about? What will the protagonist do and encounter in this volume?"

The author will give you a paragraph, which might be scattered. Your job is to **extract three elements from it**:

```
Received: "Lu Zheng is a private detective, one day Su Tang's sister comes to him and says her sister is missing. He starts to investigate, finds things are not that simple, there was a case suppressed three years ago. He investigates deeper and finds something bigger behind it, seems like someone inside the police force is playing tricks."

Extraction:
  Who → Lu Zheng (Private Detective)
  Pursuit → Find out the truth of Su Tang's disappearance
  Confrontation → Someone suppressing the case, forces inside the police force stopping him
```

**Step 2: Condense into one sentence**

Combine the three elements into one sentence and present it to the author for confirmation:

> "Let me summarize — Lu Zheng investigates the Su Tang disappearance case, discovers a suppressed old case from three years ago, investigates deeper and touches the internal forces of the police. Is that roughly right?"

**Step 3: Iterate**

| The Author Said | How to Reply |
|--------|--------|
| "Yes, that's it" | ✅ Confirmed. **Type A** → Enter 'Determine end point'; **Type B** → Skip 'Determine end point' and 'Find breakpoints', go straight to 'Determine each volume's conflict' |
| "Not quite, it should be the direction I mentioned..." | Rewrite according to the author's correction, confirm again |
| "No, the focus is not the police force, it's..." | Re-extract the three elements, present again |
| "Still a bit too broad" | "Then let's narrow it down — what specific goal does the protagonist ultimately want to achieve? Not something general like 'finding out the truth', but finding out the truth about which matter?" |

### Special Scenario Handling

| The Author Said | Analysis | How the Agent Replies |
|--------|------|-------------|
| "The protagonist wants to get stronger" | No conflict target, no confrontation | "What specific level does 'getting stronger' mean? What will they do after getting stronger? Who will they confront?" |
| "The protagonist wants revenge" | Has motivation but no path | "Who is the revenge target? How many stages will the protagonist go through to successfully get revenge?" |
| "Just one case, done in one volume" | Not a multi-volume story | Both Type A and B skip breaking down the outline, go straight to Phase 2.1 Volume direction determination |
| "I want to write a lot, several storylines" | Main storyline unclear | "Among these storylines, which one runs through from beginning to end? Are the others interspersed as side quests?" |
| **Type B and the author only has fragmented pieces** | Cannot even explain the first volume | Go back to Phase 1, chat more about settings to inspire. Tell the author "Breaking down the main storyline requires roughly knowing the direction of the first volume, it's not enough now". Manually exit Phase 2, the author comes back when ready |

## Work Backwards from the Ending

Core method: **Do not ask "how many volumes", first ask "the final scene".**

> **Type B skips this step directly.** The author doesn't know the ending, the end point is marked "TBD", breakpoints are not found, go straight into "Determine each volume's conflict".

### Step 1: Determine the End Point

**What to write:** The picture of the final scene, the protagonist's final state, the tone of the ending (BE/HE/Open-ended). Write at least one of the three.

#### Step 1: Ask about the end point

> "What is the picture of the final scene of the story in your imagination? What position does the protagonist ultimately stand in?"

**The author might give you three kinds of answers, handling methods differ:**

**Type A: Has a picture (Best)**

| The Author Said | What to Extract | Handling |
|--------|---------|------|
| "The protagonist uncovers the truth from twenty years ago, but chooses not to make it public. He continues to be his private detective, with an answer in his heart that he will never tell." | Final state (knows the truth but doesn't tell), final scene (detective agency), tone (desolate but peaceful) | ✅ Great, record as end point description |
| "The protagonist returns to the fairy realm, sits in the position he was kicked out of back then, but finds this position means bearing unwanted costs." | Final state (sits in position but at a cost), tone (tragic victory) | ✅ Great |

**Type B: Has a tone but no picture**

| The Author Said | How to Reply |
|--------|--------|
| "BE ending" | "If it's a BE ending, what happened to the protagonist in the end? Dead? Failed? Or alive but lost something?" |
| "The protagonist wins" | "What after winning? Does the world get better? What did the protagonist gain and lose? Where does the final scene stop?" |
| "Don't want it to be too cliché, but can't describe it" | "Then first exclude what you don't want — tragedy or comedy? Protagonist alive or dead? When the story ends, is the world at peace or is there a suspense left?" |

**Type C: Completely unknown**

> "That's okay, then let's not consider the ending for now. Let's chat about the content of the first volume first — feel it as you write, after the first volume is written you'll naturally know where the second volume should go."

Mark the end point as "TBD" in story.md.

#### Step 2: Verify the end point is consistent with the overall main storyline

After getting the end point, ask a follow-up question:

> "The end point sounds consistent with the main storyline direction — the protagonist ultimately {end point summary}, right?"

| End point conflicts with main storyline | How to Reply |
|--------------|--------|
| Main storyline says "uncover the truth" but end point says "protagonist does not make truth public" | "Then this is an interesting contradiction — the protagonist pursues the truth but ultimately chooses not to tell. This means there's a middle section of 'why choose not to tell'. Is this what you want?" |
| Main storyline says "defeat the demon king" but end point says "the world didn't get better" | "So victory comes at a cost? After defeating the demon king the world didn't get better, then was the protagonist's sacrifice worth it? Is this what this novel is about exploring?" |

---

### Step 2: Find Natural Breakpoints

**What to write:** How many major stages are gone through between the starting point and the end point — **not imagined out of thin air, but reasoned from "how to get from the starting point to the end point".**

#### Step 1: Ask about turning points

> "From the beginning to the ending, how many major turning points will it go through in the middle? For example, stages like 'Protagonist first discovers the truth → encounters major failure → counterattacks → final showdown'."

**Extract breakpoints from the author's answer.** The keyword is "turning point" — the boundary of each volume should be **a fundamental change**:

- Protagonist learned some key information (Information change)
- Protagonist's relationship with some party broke/established (Relationship change)
- Protagonist's strength/status leaped (Status change)
- An irreversible event occurred (Event change)

**How to judge if a "stage" is a qualified breakpoint:**

| ✅ Qualified Breakpoint | ❌ Unqualified Breakpoint |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Discover old case was suppressed" — Information change, subsequent events are different | "Protagonist continues investigating" — No change, not a breakpoint |
| "Encounter warning, someone wants to make him stop" — Relationship change, confrontation escalates | "Protagonist grows" — Too vague, what counts as grown? |
| "Protective umbrella reveals true colors" — Information change, knows who the enemy is | "Story unfolds" — Empty word, no specific change |

#### Step 2: Handle the author's answer

| The Author Said | What to Do |
|---------|--------|
| 2-4 clear stages | ✅ Each stage is the boundary of a volume |
| "Just investigate all the way to the end, no stages" | "Then in this process, what level of people will the protagonist encounter? From small fry to the mastermind behind the scenes, peeling back layer by layer? Each level might be one volume" |
| Listed 6-8 stages | Too many, merge. "Among these stages you mentioned, which ones can be merged into one volume? For example, gathering information + initial investigation can be the 'investigation stage' of one volume" |
| "Can't say, help me take a look" | Propose reasoning based on the genre, give to author for confirmation |

**Fallback (Agent Reasoning Proposal):**

If the author has no concept, use the overall main storyline + genre to guess the most likely breakpoints:

> "It sounds like your story goes from taking the case to uncovering the truth, and in the middle it goes through these stages — discovering the old case was suppressed → deep investigation encounters warning → touching the protective umbrella → final showdown. Right?"

The author might:
- "Yes, that's it" ✅
- "There is also a section..." → Add breakpoint
- "There is no warning section" → Delete, re-evaluate the remaining ones

#### Step 3: Determine the correspondence between breakpoints and volume numbers

| Number of valid breakpoints | Example | Volume Number |
|-----------|------|------|
| 0 (Done in one book) | Investigate a case to the end, no major turning points | 1 volume |
| 1 | First half gather clues, second half head-on conflict | 2 volumes |
| 2 | Beginning → Middle → End OR Intro → Development → Climax | 3 volumes |
| 3 | Beginning → Middle → Turning → End | 4 volumes |
| 4+ | Beginning → Middle 1 → Middle 2 → Turning → End OR Each volume self-contained + big main line | 5+ volumes |

**Note: This table is a reference for you to confirm whether the number of breakpoints is reasonable, not to show the author.** Do not show this table.

> **Conflict Escalation Design:** When determining the conflict for each volume, refer to the four conflict escalation techniques in `knowledge/plot-craft/index.md` (value dislocation / environmental pressure / goal displacement / chain reaction), to avoid the conflict staying in the single mode of "villain causes trouble → protagonist counterattacks".

### Step 3: Determine Each Volume's Conflict

**What to write:** The title of each volume + core conflict + estimated chapters.

#### Core Principle: Each volume is a subset of the overall main storyline

**Applies to Type A.** Understand the overall main storyline as "Who, went where, did what, had what conflict" — each volume should be a subset of this, answering "**Who, at which step of this main storyline, did what, encountered what conflict**".

Just like Journey to the West: The overall main storyline is "Tang Monk and his disciples go to the Western Heaven to obtain scriptures", the 81 tribulations are the single volumes. Each tribulation viewed individually is an event, but its meaning comes from "this is a step on the journey to obtain scriptures, only when put together can the journey be completed". **Point to the overall main storyline and ask: Without this volume, can the main storyline be completed?** — Being able to answer "cannot be completed" is what makes a qualified volume.

**This means:**
- The core conflict of each volume is not an independent sub-story, it's the **materialization** of the overall main storyline at that stage.
- The overall main storyline is "who + go where + do what + conflict", each volume is the same group of people **at this step** doing **what should be done at this step** encountering **the conflict of this step**.
- All volumes combined exactly cover the whole journey, without duplication or omission.

#### Volume Confirmation Steps

Confirm volume by volume, do not list them all at once:

> "Volume 1 'Disappearance': From taking the case to discovering the old case was suppressed, the core is investigating the Su Tang disappearance but encountering resistance. This is the first step of the main storyline — discovering the problem, but the people behind the scenes have not yet surfaced. About 10 chapters. Right?"

**After confirming the first volume, then chat about the second volume** — the order is irreversible, because the end point of the previous volume is often the starting point of the next volume.

#### Standards for filling in each field

| Field | How to Fill | ✅ Pass | ❌ Fail |
|------|--------|---------|---------|
| Title | 2-4 characters, can summarize the core event of this volume | "Disappearance", "Deep Water", "Breaking the Deadlock" | "Volume 1" (equals no title) |
| Core Conflict | **Which step of the overall main storyline +** who + doing what + hindered by what. Must be a **subset** of the overall main storyline at that stage, not starting from scratch | "Investigate Su Tang's disappearance case, discover three-year-old case was suppressed (Main storyline step 1: discover problem, but behind-the-scenes forces not yet appeared)" | "Protagonist continues investigating" (no confrontation); "Protagonist gets involved in international conspiracy" (main line is investigating disappearance, international conspiracy is another story) |
| Estimated Chapters | Estimate based on genre pacing, allow ±30% | 10, 12, 8 | 0 or empty (hasn't thought clearly about the scale) |

**The arc position is summarized at the end:** After all volumes are determined, look back at the position of each volume in the whole piece, mark functional descriptions like "Beginning - introduce mystery", "Turning/End - truth and cost". Do not ask the author to think about this when determining conflicts.

#### Allowing later volumes to be blurry / TBD

The author might only know the details of the first volume, and only have a direction for the later ones. This is normal. Handle differently based on author type:

**Type A (Has whole book concept but blurry details):**
- Later volumes must at least have **a one-sentence core conflict + floating number of chapters**.
- Refine it when getting to writing that volume.
- Do not force the author to fill in details.

**Type B (Only knows the beginning):**
- **First volume is mandatory** — title, core conflict, estimated chapters all written clearly.
- **Subsequent volumes are all marked "TBD"** — title and core_conflict written as "TBD", estimated chapters written as "?".
- The volume list in story.md only lists one volume, or lists multiple volumes but all marked "TBD".

**When returning to Phase 2:**
- Read story.md to find the next volume.
- If the next volume's core_conflict = "TBD" → skip main story outline breakdown, go straight into character voices / volume direction determination.
- After character voices are written, the direction of the next volume will naturally emerge, write into story.md replacing "TBD".

#### Structure type is a label attached at the end

After all volumes are broken down, look back:

> "Your three volumes happen to be a three-act structure — Beginning (introduce mystery), Middle (deep confrontation), Turning/End (truth and cost)."

This is an inductive label, not a starting point. Write it into the structure type field in story.md.

### Four, Complete Dialogue Example

**Scenario: Urban Suspense, author knows the beginning but not the whole picture**

```
Agent: "What is the picture of the final scene of the story in your imagination? What position does the protagonist ultimately stand in?"

Author: "I want him to be in a small office, the lights outside are on, he is sitting there reading an old case file. The case is solved, but he knows there are still many things that haven't been dug out."

Agent: "Understood — the ending is the case is solved but there is lingering resonance, the protagonist continues being a detective but has more things in his heart. Right?"

Author: "Right."

Agent: "From the beginning taking the case to this ending picture, how many major stages will it go through in the middle? For example, turning points like discovering something is wrong for the first time → encountering resistance → final showdown."

Author: "Hmm... he should first discover there's something fishy about the case, then someone warns him to stop investigating, but he continues, and finally discovers the people and things behind it."

Agent: "Sounds like three stages: discovering the old case was suppressed → deep investigation encounters warning → final showdown to uncover the truth. Right?"

Author: "Yes, roughly like that."

Agent: "Okay, then these three sections are three volumes. Let's determine the first volume: from taking the case to discovering the old case was suppressed, the core is investigating Su Tang's disappearance but encountering resistance. About how many chapters? Around 10 chapters?"

Author: "Almost, 10 chapters is fine."

Agent: "Volume two: Deep investigation, someone stops him. The core is Lu Zheng touching the protective umbrella inside the police force, every step someone is one step ahead. About 12 chapters?"
```

**Final story.md output:**

```markdown
## Main Storyline

- **Core conflict of the whole book:** Lu Zheng investigates a missing person case and discovers a twenty-year conspiracy behind it — peeling back one layer each volume, only seeing the whole picture in the last volume.
- **Structure Type:** Three-act structure
- **Total Volumes:** 3

## Volume Planning

### Volume 1: Disappearance
- **Arc Position:** Beginning - Introduce mystery
- **Core Conflict:** Investigate Su Tang's disappearance case, discover three-year-old case was suppressed
- **Estimated Chapters:** 10

### Volume 2: Deep Water
- **Arc Position:** Middle - Deep confrontation
- **Core Conflict:** Lu Zheng touches the protective umbrella inside the police force, every step someone is one step ahead
- **Estimated Chapters:** 12

### Volume 3: Breaking the Deadlock
- **Arc Position:** Turning/End - Truth and cost
- **Core Conflict:** Head-on clash with the mastermind behind the scenes, all clues converge on the incident twenty years ago
- **Estimated Chapters:** 8
```

After outline breakdown is complete, write into `story.md` according to the following template. **Type A and B output formats are the same, the difference is only in the field values.** The Agent writes directly according to this template, do not invent formats on your own.

```markdown
## Main Storyline

- **Core conflict of the whole book:** Explain the whole book conflict in one sentence — who + go where + do what + has what conflict
- **Structure Type:** Three-act structure / Four-act structure etc. (Inductive label afterwards)
- **Total Volumes:** 3

## Volume Planning

### Volume 1: Disappearance
- **Arc Position:** Beginning - Introduce mystery
- **Core Conflict:** Main storyline step 1 — who + at this step + do what + hindered by what
- **Estimated Chapters:** 10

### Volume 2: Deep Water
- **Arc Position:** Middle - Deep confrontation
- **Core Conflict:** Main storyline step 2 — same structure as above
- **Estimated Chapters:** 12

### Volume 3: Breaking the Deadlock
- **Arc Position:** Turning/End - Truth and cost
- **Core Conflict:** Main storyline step 3 — same structure as above
- **Estimated Chapters:** 8
```

### Type B Style (Only knows the beginning)

```markdown
## Main Storyline

- **Core conflict of the whole book:** TBD (Only direction for first volume is determined)
- **Structure Type:** TBD
- **Total Volumes:** ?

## Volume Planning

### Volume 1: Disappearance
- **Arc Position:** First volume (Starting point)
- **Core Conflict:** First volume direction — who + wants to do what + hindered by what
- **Estimated Chapters:** 10

### Volume 2: TBD
- **Arc Position:** TBD
- **Core Conflict:** TBD
- **Estimated Chapters:** ?
```

## How each field is used downstream

| Field | Who uses it | How it's used |
|------|------|--------|
| Core conflict of whole book (Type A) | Phase 2.1 Volume direction + Phase 2.2 Volume outline discussion | When planning each volume, ask "Which step of the overall main storyline does this volume advance?" — that is, the subset of the overall main storyline at "this step" |
| First volume direction (Type B) | Phase 2.1 Character voices | When returning to Phase 2, after character voices deduce the next volume, replace "TBD" |
| Arc Position | Phase 2.1 Template selection/Character voices | The "Beginning" stage mainly focuses on introduction; the "Turning" volume pacing should speed up |
| Core Conflict | Phase 2.2 Volume outline discussion | Directly written into this volume's `volume.md`. Should be able to pass the "Overall main storyline → Volume by volume" and "Volume → Reverse deduction" in the acceptance three-way check |
| Estimated Chapters | Phase 2.2 Chapter number allocation | Total reference for allocating chapter numbers for each stage |
| Structure Type | Cross-volume pacing perception (Reference only) | A label summarized afterwards, not a basis for planning |

## Acceptance: Three-Way Check

The core of acceptance is not whether the fields are filled — it's **pointing to one and asking if the other matches**. Type A/B share the same method, differences are marked.

### Check 1: Overall Main Storyline → Volume by Volume (Top-Down Relationship)

Point to the overall main storyline (Type B: First volume direction), ask for each volume:

> **"Are the title, story, and core conflict of this volume placed at which step of the overall main storyline?"**

- ✅ Can answer ("This volume corresponds to the investigation stage, it's the first step of the main line")
- ❌ Cannot answer or far-fetched → This volume is disconnected from the main storyline

**Type B:** Only check the first volume. Ask "Is this the starting point? Does it make sense for the story to start here?"

### Check 2: Volume Sequence → Complete Path (Sequential Relationship)

Line up the core conflicts of all volumes, without looking at the overall main storyline, ask:

> **"Combined together, are these conflicts a complete storyline? Are there skipped steps or breaks?"**

- ✅ Combined is a natural story progression, the next volume is the natural result of the previous volume
- ❌ Combined is not a single line → Some volume is not a subset of the main storyline, or a key transition is missing

Example: Volume 1 "Discover old case suppressed" → Volume 2 "Deep investigation encounters resistance" → Volume 3 "Clash with mastermind behind scenes" → Combined is a complete investigation process ✅. Volume 1 "Take case" → Volume 2 "Protagonist returns to hometown to visit relatives" → Volume 3 "Showdown" → Broken in the middle ❌.

**Type B:** Only has one volume, this check automatically passes.

### Check 3: Volumes → Reverse Deduce Overall Main Storyline (Convergence Relationship)

Only look at the core conflicts of all volumes (do not look at the overall main storyline), ask:

> **"After reading the conflicts of these volumes, can you guess what the core conflict of the whole book roughly is?"**

- ✅ Can be deduced (each volume is a small piece of the big story, combined they point to the same big goal)
- ❌ Cannot be deduced (volume conflicts are independent of each other, do not converge into the same main storyline)

To put it plainly: if the conflicts of each volume point in completely different directions when strung together (one volume investigating a case, one volume falling in love, one volume business war), they are not the same main storyline.

**Type B:** Only has one volume, skip this check.

---

### Field Completeness Check (Auxiliary)

After completing the three-way check, quickly check the field integrity. All three-way verification passed but there are missing fields → Complete them. The three-way check fails → **Fix the main relationship first and then fill in the fields**, the order is irreversible.

| Check Item | Type A | Type B |
|--------|--------|--------|
| The core conflict of the whole book / The direction of the first volume | Contains "who + pursuit + confrontation", and you can't finish the answer in one volume | Contains "who + what you want to do + what is hindered by what", marked "Only the direction of the first volume" |
| End direction | Record (screen/tone/status, at least one) | Allow "pending" |
| Volume field | Each volume has title/core conflict/estimated chapters | The first volume is required, and subsequent volumes allow "pending" |
| Conflict format | "What are the steps of the main line + who + does + what is hindered by what" | "Who + wants to do + what is hindered by what" |

**General Inspector:** Pointing to the main line and asking "Without this volume, can the main line be completed?" - Can't finish it ✅
