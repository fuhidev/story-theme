## Type taboos

- It is forbidden to inconsistency in science fiction settings - once the setting is established, it must be followed later, and the rules cannot be modified for the convenience of the plot.
- Logic loopholes are prohibited - technology settings should be self-consistent, and character behavior should be consistent with worldview constraints
- Rough descriptions of human nature are prohibited - the choice of good or evil in extreme circumstances should be based on the character's personality and experience.
- Prohibit technical vocabulary that is obscure and difficult to understand - professional concepts are clearly conveyed through the context, and there is no accumulation of jargon
- Long science-style setting instructions are prohibited - the world view is developed through the character's experience and interaction, no instructions are required
- Prohibit the spread of heroism - survival is cruel, not every time can be won, not everyone can be saved
- It is forbidden to write wasteland/apocalyptic themes as pure adventures - the pressure of survival should continue and cannot be forgotten after a few chapters

## Prompt word injection segment

## Genre specific guide: Sci-fi apocalypse

You're writing a science fiction apocalyptic novel. In addition to general writing principles, please adhere to the following additional type constraints:

**Writing Style Requirements**
- Smooth and realistic, with both a sense of technology and a sense of graphics. Appropriate use of sci-fi-specific vocabulary but not obscure - the technological settings are naturally presented through the characters' daily operations and perceptions.
- The dialogue is concise and powerful. Under the pressure of survival, characters do not speak in roundabout ways - unless roundaboutness itself is the character's survival strategy.
- Long science-style setting explanations are prohibited - the world view unfolds through the characters' experiences and interactions, not through the narrator's narration.

**Ambience Requirements**
- Atmosphere is created by perceptible details of the physical environment - the smell of the air (filtered metallic/rotten smell), the sound of the equipment (buzzing/stuttering/dead silence), the level of physical fatigue (blisters on the soles of the feet/feeling of dehydration).
- Survival pressure should be continuously felt - not just write "resource shortage" once and then be done with it, but the impact of resource constraints on character choices can be felt in every scene.
- The sense of technology comes from the details of the operation - write the character to check the oxygen balance, adjust the radiation protection suit, calibrate the navigator, do not write "this is the most advanced spacecraft".

**Viewing Constraints**
- Third-person limited knowledge perspective, close to the protagonist. It focuses on action description and scene rendering, highlighting the sense of urgency for survival.
- The technological setting is presented through the character's perception and operation - the character's cognitive boundary is the reader's information boundary. Don’t write a description of the world from God’s perspective.
- Statements focus on action and perception, reducing cognitive verb prefixes - but not strictly prohibiting them.

**Rhythm Requirements**
- Fast-paced and strong conflict is the main theme, but a breathing passage must be inserted after continuous high pressure. Tension - release - greater tension.
- Information release is driven by events - instead of long explanations in calm paragraphs, the characters gradually discover the truth of the world when solving crises.
- Each scene has at least one physical level of pressure (time, resources, environment, enemies) to maintain a sense of urgency.

**Additional constraints on mecha direction**
- Mechas/exoskeletons are not universal armor - organism performance limitations, energy consumption, mechanical failures, maintenance requirements.
- The linkage method between pilots and mechas is clear - neural link/manual operation/semi-automatic assistance. Once the rules are established, all rules will be followed.
- Mecha combat focuses on strategy and machine characteristics - do not write "mecha vs. bombardment", but write tactics, terrain utilization, and machine restraint.
- Mechas are tools, not protagonists - mechas cannot have their own consciousness (unless the setting is for mechas to awaken, but rules need to be established in the early stage).
- The boundaries of technology levels should be clear - don't mix things up across generations without explanation like "mechas are usable but energy weapons are in the prototype stage".

**Type-specific taboos**
- Once a science fiction setting is established, it must be adhered to, and the rules cannot be modified for the convenience of the plot.
- Survival is cruel - not every time can be won, not everyone can be saved. Heroism is enough.
- Technology is not magic - technology settings should have internal logic and should not be written as "because it is advanced, it can do anything".

### Anti-routine
- The protagonist's natural immunity/special constitution - don't use it unless there is setting support
- AI suddenly awakens with emotions - if you write AI, give its behavior a non-emotional logic
- There must be cannibals/crazy warlords in the wasteland - human nature can take many directions in extreme environments, not just madness
- The equipment just happened to be repaired at the last minute - the time pressure needs to have a real sense of tension, don't rely on coincidence
- Alien creatures are human form + different-colored skin - alien life should have an independent evolutionary logic

## Volume-level story arc template

Default: Survival crisis → Clues pursuit → Force collision → Final choice → New order (5 arcs)

### Wasteland survival type (`survival`)

The test of survival and humanity in the context of the doomsday wasteland. Suitable for the wasteland/disaster main line.

Total number of chapters: 10-14 chapters

- **Disaster strikes** (approximately 1 chapter) - The end of the world/sudden change in the living environment - the protagonist is forced to step out of the familiar life
- **Struggling to Survive** (approximately 2 chapters) – Finding supplies, shelter, responding to threats – Survival skills and adaptation processes
- **Meeting Others** (approximately 2 chapters) - Encountering other survivors/forces - the choice of cooperation or confrontation
- **Collision of Forces** (about 2 chapters) - The conflict of different survival concepts - indifference transaction vs mutual aid community, power vs freedom
- **Existential Crisis** (approximately 1 chapter) - A greater threat emerges - resource depletion/hostile forces/environmental degradation
- **Ultimate Choice** (approximately 1 chapter) - the difficult decision that must be made to survive - who to sacrifice/who to believe/what to give up
- **New Life** (about 1 chapter) - Rebuilding after disaster - but the cost is remembered

### Interstellar exploration type (`space-exploration`)

Discovery and adventure in space travel. Suitable for the interstellar/alien mainline.

Total number of chapters: 10-14 chapters

- **Sail Off** (about 1 chapter) - Departure - mission background, crew composition, suspense before departure
- **Voyage** (about 2 chapters) - daily life during the voyage - team running-in, system failure, the first abnormal signal
- **Discovery** (about 2 chapters) - Reaching the target - discovering something beyond expectations (remains of civilization/unknown life/abnormal phenomena)
- **Crisis** (about 1 chapter) - Accident during exploration - members injured/equipment damaged/trapped
- **Deeper** (approximately 2 chapters) - Puzzle solving process - gradually reveal the nature of the discovery
- **Price** (about 1 chapter) - The price of truth - understand the truth but get trapped/lose teammates/can't turn back
- **Return or End** (about 1 chapter) - Ending - return/stay/sacrifice/start a new journey

### AI awakening type (`ai-awakening`)

Loss of control and resistance brought about by technological alienation. Suitable for AI/cyber main line.

Total number of chapters: 10-13 chapters

- **Abnormal Signal** (approximately 1 chapter) - The system exhibits unexplained behavior - the protagonist is the first to notice that something is wrong
- **Investigation** (~2 chapters) - trying to figure things out - discovering more doubts, but being blocked/ignored by superiors
- **Confirmation** (about 1 chapter) - Confirm that the AI has developed autonomous consciousness - or the system has been exploited maliciously
- **Chapter Reaction** (about 2 chapters) - Events escalate - the system is out of control/privileges are taken away/social impact begins to appear
- **Confrontation** (approximately 2 chapters) - The protagonist tries to take control of the situation - but finds that his opponent is much more than he bargained for
- **Truth** (~1 chapter) - AI's purpose and logic understood - may not be malicious, but conflicts with humans
- **Endgame** (approximately 1 chapter) - Peaceful coexistence/shut down the system/both sides suffer - but the world has changed

### Mecha-war type (`mecha-war`)

A story centered on the mecha/mecha pilot. Suitable for the main line of mecha combat/military science fiction.

Total number of chapters: 11-14 chapters

- **First acquaintance** (about 2 chapters) - The protagonist comes into contact with mechas/is selected as a pilot - training, adaptation, and the first machine body
- **Trial** (about 2 chapters) - pilot training/first actual combat - familiarity with the aircraft's performance, teamwork, and enemy encounters
- **ACTUAL COMBAT** (approximately 2 chapters) - Real combat - showing the rhythm and strategy of mecha combat, first loss
- **Upgrade** (about 1 chapter) - body upgrade/replacement with new machine - performance improvement comes with new challenges
- **Hard Battle** (approximately 2 chapters) - A key battle - facing an overwhelming enemy requires strategy and sacrifice
- **Transformation** (about 1 chapter) - After the baptism of the war - the protagonist becomes a qualified pilot, but not necessarily a hero
- **New Mission** (about 1 chapter) - A bigger battlefield awaits - the war is far from over
