## Blueprint of Writing Style

### Voice

Colloquial, life-oriented, not literary or artistic. The sentences alternate between short and medium sentences, in line with the rhythm of urban life.
The dialogue fits the character's background - the programmer's speaking style is different from that of the salesperson, the demolition households and the professor
The wording is different. The narrative is close to the protagonist's occupation and cognitive level - police perspective, doctor perspective,
The world seen from the perspective of a delivery boy is different. It is not written in the intellectual tone of the author himself.


### Atmosphere

The real texture of the city - the noise and loneliness of the city, the distance between people, steel and concrete
The smoke of fireworks inside. Three background colors are available: warm and cold for daily use - the temperature at work, at home, and in the neighborhood;
Gray areas - gangsters, marginalized figures, the other side of the urban underground;
Glimmer of ideals - the struggle and persistence of ordinary people in big cities.
The core is "truth" - not suspended, not pretentious, making readers feel like "this is the city I live in."


### Type elements

- Modern urban background (real city or fictional city, with specific details of life)
- Occupational scene (the character’s occupation is not a decoration, the work content is realistic)
- Complex urban interpersonal relationships (multiple lines of work/family/friends/love/neighborhood intertwined)
- Realistic pressure (house prices/competition in the workplace/pressure on marriage/intergenerational conflicts)
- Fireworks - street stalls, convenience stores, rental houses, late-night drinking establishments
- Differences in social class (the city is the stage with the clearest distinction between classes)


**Perspective:** third_person_limited

Third-person limited knowledge perspective, close to the protagonist. The viewing angle is stable and does not jump. The core of urban themes is the sense of substitution——
What the protagonist sees, hears, and feels is the world experienced by the reader. mental activity through
Actions and details are externalized, and no large internal monologues are written.

## Type taboos

- No sense of suspension - the character's income, consumption, and social circle should conform to their settings, and they cannot live on a flat floor with a monthly salary of 5,000.
- Literary accents are prohibited - do not write 'The city's neon is like tears'. Urban beauty comes from real details, not rhetoric
- No elite accent - not all protagonists are CEOs/magnificent people. Ordinary people also have ordinary people’s stories
- Professional common sense mistakes are prohibited - you should have a basic understanding of the industry in which the character is located, and you cannot distort industry common sense for the sake of the plot.
- No dialogue that is divorced from reality - no gimmicks like 'this woman is so interesting'. Conversations should be like real people speaking.
- Forcible sensationalism is prohibited - the emotional power of the city comes from the accumulation of real details, not long paragraphs of lyricism

## Prompt word injection segment

## Genre specific guide: Urban

You're writing urban fiction. In addition to general writing principles, please adhere to the following additional type constraints:

**Writing Style Requirements**
- Colloquial, life-oriented, not literary or artistic. The sentences alternate between short and medium sentences, in line with the rhythm of urban life.
- Dialogue sounds like a real person speaking - with catchphrases, half-words, hesitations and repetitions. Characters speak in ways that fit their profession and background.
- Do not write literary and artistic rhetoric such as "the city's neon lights are like tears". The beauty of the city comes from real details: the lights of convenience stores, the subway during the evening rush hour, and the security booths in the community.

**Scenario Requirements**
- The scene has specific life details - not just "they met in a cafe", but the fan in the corner, the old songs playing on the bar, and the coffee stains on the rim of the cup.
- The professional scenes have a sense of reality - the characters' jobs are not decorations, and they have professional judgments about their own industries.
- Income, consumption level and role setting match - the scope of activities with a monthly salary of 5,000 is not the top level of China World Trade Center Phase 3.

**Viewing Constraints**
- Third-person limited knowledge perspective, close to the protagonist. Mental activity is externalized through actions and details.
- The perspective is stable and does not jump - readers see the city through the eyes of the protagonist.

**Rhythm Requirements**
- Daily narrative is the main focus, and the rhythm is adjusted according to the variant type (see variant file).
- Daily scenes are not watery - each scene fulfills at least one function: promoting relationships, burying information, creating contrast, and enhancing emotions.
- The amount of information in the dialogue is more important than the narration - the characters' true words are spoken over the wine table, after a quarrel, and in late-night text messages.

**Type-specific taboos**
- No sense of suspension - the details of life can withstand scrutiny.
- Don’t write in an elite accent – ​​not all protagonists are CEOs.
- Mistake of not writing about career - Do your homework on the industry you are writing about.
- Don't write in a literary tone - the urban appeal comes from truth, not rhetoric.

## Volume-level story arc template

Default: Life → Contradiction → Dilemma → Choice → Growth → New Life (6 arcs)

### Daily-life (`daily-life`)

Growth and change in urban life. Universal structure.

Total number of chapters: 11-14 chapters

- **Daily** (about 2 chapters) - The protagonist's life status and dilemma - work/family/relationships
- **Ups and Downs** (about 2 chapters) - An event breaks the ordinary - new job/new neighbor/unexpected reunion
- **Conflict** (about 2 chapters) - the chain reaction caused by the incident - workplace conflict/emotional entanglement/realistic pressure
- **Choice** (approx. 1 chapter) - Having to choose between several options - there is no perfect answer
- **Action** (about 2 chapters) – actions and consequences of choices – difficulties and surprises in execution
- **Growth** (about 1 chapter) - changes after experiencing the incident - the protagonist has a new understanding of life
- **New Daily** (approximately 1 chapter) - New life after changes - old worries are solved, new challenges arise
